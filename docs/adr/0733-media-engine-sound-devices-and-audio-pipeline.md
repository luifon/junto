# 0733. Media engine: its crate, the sound devices and the audio pipeline

## Status

Accepted

Open: the engine was built and tested on macOS and in a Linux container with virtual sound devices (ADR 0750). The explicit native macOS tier checks the production capture and playout paths through BlackHole (ADR 0913). The Windows build was not run. Physical microphones and physical audio outputs still require operator verification.

## Context

ADR 0701 fixes the LiveKit Rust SDK as the call implementation and names it as the provider of audio devices, echo cancellation, noise suppression and gain control. The client core has every call route and no media (ADR 0720). ADR 0507 requires a level meter per person, a volume per person for each listener and push-to-talk; ADR 0503 requires a sound block that the client applies. ADR 0902 requires tests without a real microphone and without sound from the loudspeakers.

The SDK (`livekit` 0.9.4) offers two ways to carry sound:

- Its platform device module opens the microphone and the output device itself. It plays every remote track through one mix inside the WebRTC library, and the SDK has no call that sets the volume of one remote track. A test cannot give it a device, so the path of the product would not be the path of the tests.
- Its audio source and audio stream take and give PCM frames, and the audio processing module of its WebRTC library (echo cancellation, noise suppression, gain control) can be used on such frames.

## Decision

Crate:

- The crate `junto-media` holds the media engine and the calls of a client (ADR 0734). It depends on `junto-client-core` and on the SDK, and has no dependency on gpui. The desktop client depends on it; the client core and `junto-cli` do not, so they do not link the WebRTC library.
- The SDK is pinned at `livekit` 0.9.4, as the step 0 participant used it. The workspace and the standalone test participant use its build inputs from `vendor/`, with the corrections that this ADR names (publisher negotiation, the order of mute requests, the mute state after a resume, the negotiation after a close, the copy of the session descriptions, the processing rate, the scale of held planes and the software fallback of a hardware encoder, below). Its WebRTC library is downloaded once, when the crate is built first.
- A binary that links the crate passes `-ObjC` to the linker on macOS, in its build script. Without it the binary stops at its start.

Sound devices:

- The engine opens sound devices through one interface: list the devices, open an input, open an output. The product implements it with `cpal` 0.18 (CoreAudio, WASAPI, PipeWire). The tests implement it with a tone as the microphone, a sink that keeps what it was given, and a device list that a test changes.
- The device module of the SDK is not used. This changes ADR 0701: the SDK provides the processing, and `cpal` provides the devices.
- On Linux the PipeWire host of `cpal` is used, so the sound of a call is played by the client's own PipeWire connection (ADR 0502). The device list and subsequent stream opens use one host snapshot. A failed PipeWire initialisation or connection is reported, without falling back to ALSA or PulseAudio (ADR 0750).
- A chosen device is used while it is connected. When it is not, the default of the system is opened, and the choice stays stored. A microphone that is opened in this way during a call, in the place of the microphone that the call used before (after a failure, at a new media connection, or after a block), sends nothing until the person opens it (ADR 0794). The chosen device that is connected again is used without that.
- A stream that fails is opened again once, on the device that the rule gives then. When the stream that was opened in this way fails within 5 seconds, the device stays closed and its failure is shown. A microphone is opened again when the person asks for it or chooses a device; an output when the person chooses a device or changes a sound setting. A microphone that could not be opened is not tried again by itself either.
- A device is opened with its default format when the pipeline converts the rate of that format. Otherwise it is opened at another rate that it offers, 48 kHz first, with the channels and the sample type of the default where it offers them. A device without such a rate is not opened.
- A call into the device interface can wait for the sound system: an open lists and configures the device, and on Linux it takes the lock of the one PipeWire host, which a listing of the devices or an open of the engine can hold. Such a call is made only on a thread of the engine, of the runtime of the client, of a device or of a player, and never on the thread of a window. The tone of an incoming call is the one device that the window starts: its player (`audio::ring::RingPlayer`) opens and closes the output on a thread of its own and follows the newest request there. A request only sends the wanted state (silence, or the tone on a device) and returns at once. An output that did not open is tried again at the next request for the tone. Tests: `audio::ring::tests::a_device_that_is_slow_to_open_does_not_hold_the_caller` gives the player an output that opens only when the test lets it, and fails when the request has not returned within 10 seconds; it failed with the player that opened the output in the request. `a_device_that_did_not_open_is_tried_again_at_the_next_request` and `ring_sound_uses_the_injected_output_and_stops` check the retry and the close.
- The engine reads the microphone permission of the system before it opens a microphone. `not asked`: it makes the system ask, and opens the microphone after a positive answer. `refused`: it opens no microphone. The permission is behind an interface; the product reads it through AVFoundation on macOS, and a system without such a permission counts as allowed.

Pipeline:

- Every stage works at 48 kHz in frames of 10 ms, which the processing module and the audio source of the SDK take.
- The processing module works at 48 kHz inside. The default of its WebRTC version (`maximum_internal_processing_rate`) is 32 kHz: the module then resamples each frame of the microphone and of the playout from 48 kHz to 32 kHz and back, and removes everything above 16 kHz from the microphone. The vendored `webrtc-sys` sets 48 kHz in `include/livekit/apm.h`; at 48 kHz the module splits each frame into three bands of 16 kHz without a resampler. Test: `audio::processing::tests::the_module_keeps_the_microphone_above_16_khz` gives the module with the echo canceller a tone of 18 kHz and fails when the tone loses more than 6 dB; it lost all of the tone with the default. At 48 kHz the module has no resamplers, which saves more processor time than its third band costs; the timing `timing::the_processing_of_one_frame_of_each_direction` measures one frame of each direction, and `docs/client-performance.md` has its figures at both rates. The correction is removed when the default of the pinned WebRTC version is 48 kHz or a pinned upstream version sets it.
- The conversion between a device and the pipeline works in steps that are a whole number of samples on both sides, so the time of the device and the time of the pipeline stay equal. A step is 10 ms at a rate that 100 divides, such as 44.1 kHz and 48 kHz, 20 ms at 22.05 kHz and 40 ms at 11.025 kHz. The rates from 8 kHz to 192 kHz with a step of at most 40 ms are converted. The resampler of the SDK takes 10 ms at a time; a longer step is given to it as 10 ms at rates that are as many times higher, which is the same ratio.
- The capture, the playout and the sound of a share give each step the samples of its time whatever the resampler returns: a shorter result is filled with silence and a longer one is cut. The playout fills the buffer of the device in a loop of steps, which therefore ends. Test: `audio::rate::tests::the_resampler_gives_a_whole_step_for_every_rate_that_the_pipeline_converts` converts one step of every rate that the pipeline converts, in both directions, with one and two channels and from two channels to one, and checks that the resampler gives the whole step; it does for each of the 3,521 rates.
- Microphone: the samples of the device become mono (the mean of its first two channels) and are resampled; the processing module processes each frame; the level is measured; the frame is sent, or replaced by silence while the microphone is muted or push-to-talk waits for its key; the audio source of the published track takes the frame. The audio source applies no processing of its own.
- Sensitivity of voice activation: without push to talk, the person can set a threshold from -60 to -12 dBFS, the range of the input meter (`voice_threshold` in `voice.json`, absent by default, which sends all of the open microphone). A frame whose level after the processing reaches the threshold is sent, and so are the next 30 frames (300 ms), so that the quiet ends of words and the short pauses between them are sent; other frames are sent as silence, as with a closed gate, and the person is not marked as speaking. The input meter still measures them. The voice settings show "Sensitivity" with a toggle; when it is on, a slider over the range of the meter shows the live level of the microphone and the threshold as its thumb, as Discord does. Outside a call the settings open the chosen input with the chosen processing while they are shown (`Calls::start_input_test`, on a thread of the runtime of the client, never on the thread of a window): its frames end at the meter and nothing is sent. It is not opened without the permission of the system, so opening the settings never shows the question of the system; it is closed when the settings close, when another layer replaces them and when a call is entered, and it is opened again when the device or the processing changes. A start or stop that comes after an open on its way closes that open again. The arrow keys move the threshold by 1 dB. The threshold is used at once and written to the file at the release of the slider. Reason: voice activation sent every sound of an open microphone, including the noise of a room. Tests: `audio::capture::tests::voice_activation_sends_only_sound_above_the_threshold` (a quiet tone is sent as silence, a louder one is sent with the hold, and nothing is held without a threshold), `calls::tests::the_level_test_shows_the_input_outside_a_call_until_it_stops` (the level of a tone arrives outside a call, the stop clears it, and without the permission nothing opens and nothing asks), and the window test `calls::the_sound_settings_of_the_application_are_kept_over_a_restart` (the level shows in the settings outside a call, and the microphone closes with the settings).
- Up to 500 ms of frames wait when the media connection takes them late. The mute and the key are read when a frame is made and again when the connection takes it. A frame that waited is sent as silence when the microphone is muted or the key is released at that time, and also when either happened at any time since the frame was made.
- Playout: each remote audio track is read as stereo frames into a queue of its own. The output device asks for samples; the mixer takes 10 ms of each queue, multiplies by the volume of the person (0 to 200 %), adds, and limits the sum. The mix is given to the processing module as the reference of the echo canceller and is converted to the format of the device.
- A queue starts to play when it holds 30 ms, and again after it ran empty. It never holds more than 200 ms; older sound is dropped.
- Sound off, by the person or by a sound block (ADR 0503), plays silence and still takes the tracks, so that nothing old is played afterwards.
- The level of a source rises at once and falls by 0.6 dB per frame. A meter is empty at -60 dBFS and full at -12 dBFS. A person speaks while the level of what the microphone sends is above -45 dBFS, and for 400 ms after it.
- A person can turn each part of the processing off. With all three off, the frames pass unchanged. While at least one part is on, the high-pass filter of the module is on too: it removes hum below speech, and the person has no setting for it.

Engine:

- One engine is one media connection. One task owns the room, the microphone track, the remote tracks and the two devices; a caller sends it commands and reads two values that it keeps current: the state and the levels.
- The microphone is open while the device is in the room, no microphone block is told by the server (ADR 0734), and the permission of the participant in the room allows a microphone track. The server puts a microphone block into the join token and into that permission (ADR 0510), so the block holds from the first moment of a connection: a blocked person who enters again opens no microphone. The microphone track is published while the microphone is open. A block withdraws the track and closes the microphone; a mute keeps both and sends silence, and is told to the room as a muted track.
- A publication that the media server refuses is tried again every second, while the microphone may be sent.
- When the call library connects anew, it withdraws the microphone track and publishes it again under another identifier. The engine waits for that track for 10 seconds and keeps the audio source that the track is bound to. The track that returns with the identifier of the withdrawn one is the microphone track again; another one is withdrawn. A track that does not return is published anew while the microphone may be sent.
- When the engine leaves a room, it closes both devices first and then waits at most 3 seconds for the media server to confirm the leave.
- The call library restores an interrupted connection by itself. When it gives up, or the media server ends the connection, the engine closes both devices and waits for a new token (ADR 0734).

Executors:

- The window polls the futures of the crate on the executor of gpui, which has no Tokio runtime (ADR 0715). A future that needs a runtime and is polled outside one panics; on the main thread of the window the panic ends the program.
- Any executor can poll a future that a public function of `Calls` returns: `share_sources`, `join`, `join_invited`, `leave`, and the test functions `statistics` and `sound_check`. Such a future waits only for a channel of `tokio::sync` or for a task, which need no runtime to be polled. `Engine::close` also waits only for a channel, and so do the receivers of `tokio::sync::watch` that `Calls` gives for its view and its pictures. The work that needs Tokio (its timers, `tokio::spawn`, its network types and the calls of the call library) runs in tasks of the runtime of the client.
- `Calls::share_sources` calls the source list of the capture in a task of that runtime on every system, and returns a future that waits for the task. Dropping the future ends the task. When the runtime has shut down, the future ends with an error that states that the task was cancelled; it neither panics nor waits.
- The functions of the device interfaces that return a future (`ScreenCapture::sources` and `start`, `CameraCapture::request_access` and `start`, `MicrophonePermission::request`) are called, and their futures polled, only by the crate and only in tasks of the runtime of the client. Their implementations may use Tokio: the macOS capture bounds its wait for the system with a Tokio timer, and the Linux capture uses D-Bus connections and blocking tasks of the runtime. The desktop client does not call these functions; it uses `Calls`.
- The synthetic devices of `test-support` check in each of their futures that it runs inside a Tokio runtime, and panic otherwise. A window test or a call test then fails where the crate polls the future of a device interface outside the runtime of the client, also when the place would work with the device of one system and not with the device of another.
- Tests: `calls::tests::every_future_of_the_calls_ends_on_an_executor_without_a_tokio_runtime` polls each of these futures with `futures_executor::block_on` on a thread without a runtime, and fails when they have not ended after 30 seconds. Its capture waits with a Tokio timer, as the macOS capture does. Its server is known to the client and answers nothing, so `join` and `join_invited` take their turn on the runtime and their futures wait for the refusal. `statistics` and `sound_check` ask the engine only in a call: the media live tier polls them, together with `join`, `join_invited` of an accepted invitation, `share_sources` and `leave`, on a thread without a runtime in a real call with a share (`executor::the_futures_of_the_calls_end_on_an_executor_without_a_tokio_runtime`). The window test `calls::the_futures_of_the_calls_end_on_the_executor_of_the_window` polls `share_sources`, `join`, `join_invited` and `leave` on the executor of gpui against the server of the tests, which refuses the joins; the window does not call the two test functions. `executor::tests::dropping_the_future_ends_the_task` and `calls::tests::a_reading_of_the_sources_ends_as_cancelled_when_the_runtime_has_shut_down` check the end of the task. The native window stage `w1` lists the screens of macOS and starts a share of one through the window (ADR 0913).

Native connection shutdown:

- The native peer-connection wrapper calls `PeerConnectionInterface::Close` synchronously in its destructor, before releasing the interface, observer or factory. The call waits for the signaling thread while the interface still has a positive reference count. Pending callbacks can finish before `Close` unregisters the observer and stops further callbacks. The [pinned implementation](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/89d790b/pc/peer_connection.cc#L1689-L1766) invalidates pending description callbacks and clears the observer before returning.
- This order also applies when runtime shutdown cancels a task, a connection fails during setup, or a close future is dropped. An asynchronous room leave cannot provide that guarantee during runtime shutdown.
- Without this order, the last release enters `PeerConnectionProxyWithInternal` destruction on the runtime thread while the signaling thread can still execute `OnTrack`. That callback copies the wrapper's interface reference to an `RtpTransceiver`; its `AddRef` then calls a pure virtual function on the partially destroyed proxy. The wrapper's observer is also still registered during member destruction.
- The workspace uses the build inputs of `webrtc-sys` 0.3.48 from `vendor/`, with this destructor correction. The WebRTC binary revision is unchanged. The patch is removed when a pinned upstream version guarantees this order. Source provenance and the retained licence notices are kept beside the dependency.
- Native tests observe the closed-state callback on last-owner release, cancellation during runtime shutdown, and a stopped transceiver that outlives its wrapper. Dropping one of several owners does not close the connection; an explicit close followed by destruction remains valid. These checks use no media server or real device.

Session descriptions of a native connection:

- The wrapper reads a session description of a connection (current, pending, local or remote) and copies it on the signaling thread of WebRTC, in one call. The proxy of the connection reads a description on the signaling thread and returns a pointer to the description that the connection owns; the published wrapper copied it on the calling thread. The signaling thread replaces and frees the descriptions at each negotiation, so a copy on another thread could read a freed description.
- The call library reads the remote description from a task of its runtime at each candidate of the signal connection (`PeerTransport::add_ice_candidate`). A run of the media live tier ended with SIGSEGV in that copy: the core of the test process gave `webrtc::Codec::Codec` in `SessionDescriptionInterface::Clone`, called from `livekit_ffi::PeerConnection::current_remote_description` on a Tokio worker thread, under `SessionInner::on_signal_event`.
- Test: `engine::tests::the_descriptions_can_be_read_while_negotiations_replace_them` reads the descriptions of two connections from four threads while 300 negotiations with ICE restarts replace them on the signaling thread. The negotiations bind sockets on the network interfaces, so the test does not run on Windows, where a new program that does so opens a prompt of the firewall on the desktop (ADR 0915).
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version copies the descriptions on the signaling thread.

Pictures of the own tracks:

- The engine gives each picture of the own camera and the own share to the SDK with its capture time, in microseconds of a monotonic clock of the process, never zero (`engine::sent::frame`). For a picture with the time zero the SDK reads the system clock and unwraps its difference to 1970, which panics for a clock before 1970; the call runs in the delegates of the capture, where a panic ends the program. The SDK maps the time of each picture to the clock of its WebRTC library. The SDK also sends black pictures from a task of its own every 100 ms until the first picture of a source arrives (`raw_keepalive_task` of `libwebrtc` 0.3.51), and takes their time from the system clock with the same unwrap; with a clock before 1970 that task panics and ends, the panic stays in the task, and the source works without it. That case is accepted: the SDK changes only through a vendored copy of `libwebrtc`, and its effect is the end of the black pictures before the first picture. Tests: `engine::sent::tests::a_sent_frame_has_a_capture_time_that_is_not_zero_and_does_not_go_back`, and `engine::sent::tests::every_frame_of_an_own_track_is_made_with_a_capture_time`, which reads the sources of the engine and fails when the engine outside its test modules makes a frame with `VideoFrame::new`.

Scale of held planes:

- `include/livekit/yuv_helper.h` and `src/yuv_helper.rs` of the vendored `webrtc-sys` add `i420_scale`: the scale of libyuv (`I420Scale`) with the box filter of `webrtc::I420Buffer::ScaleFrom`, from planes that the caller holds and does not give up. `I420Buffer::scale` of the SDK scales only planes that it may change, so a received picture had to be copied before it was scaled (ADR 0774). The module `share::picture::scale` calls it. Tests: `share::picture::tests::held_planes_are_scaled_as_the_call_library_scales_them` compares its result with `I420Buffer::scale`, byte for byte, and `share::picture::tests::held_planes_drawn_smaller_are_not_copied_first` fails when the picture is copied into new planes before the scale. Removed when a pinned upstream version scales planes that it reads only.

Software fallback of a hardware encoder:

- An encoder of a hardware backend, such as VideoToolbox (ADR 0737), can fail to start, for example when the system cannot make its compression session. The published `webrtc-sys` made the simulcast adapter of WebRTC without a fallback factory, and its encoder selector then asks for the same backend again, so the track stayed published and sent no picture. The vendored copy makes every encoder through `CreateAdaptedEncoder` (`src/video_encoder_factory.cpp`): for a format that asks for a hardware backend, the adapter gets a factory of the software encoders of WebRTC, and WebRTC wraps each hardware encoder in its software fallback. A hardware encoder that does not start or that fails is replaced by the software encoder of the same codec (OpenH264 for H.264, libvpx for VP8; the WebRTC archives of macOS, Windows and Linux are built with `rtc_use_h264`), so the track keeps its codec and its receivers notice nothing. Software and pass-through backends get no fallback.
- Test: `encoder_fallback::a_hardware_encoder_that_does_not_start_falls_back_to_the_software_encoder_of_its_codec` makes an encoder of H.264 and of VP8 with the backend VideoToolbox through `CreateAdaptedEncoder`, from a factory whose encoders do not start, through the test bridge `src/encoder_fallback.rs` and `src/encoder_fallback.cpp`; it encodes one picture and fails unless the software encoder of the codec encoded it. It runs on every system in the workspace suite.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version gives the simulcast adapter a software fallback.

Exceptions of the native wrapper:

- A function of `webrtc-sys` reports a failure with a C++ exception, and the bridge function of `cxx` catches it and copies `what()` into a Rust `String`. The published crate throws `std::runtime_error(text)`, where `text` is a `std::string` that the throw expression makes (16 places in `src/` and the libyuv macro of `include/livekit/yuv_helper.h`, among them an invalid ICE server in `create_peer_connection`, an invalid session description in `create_session_description` and an invalid candidate in `create_ice_candidate`). The SDK reaches these places with descriptions and candidates from the media server.
- The WebRTC library for Windows is built with `_HAS_EXCEPTIONS=0`, and the build script of `webrtc-sys` gives every define of `webrtc.ninja` to the C++ of the crate and to its bridge code. With that value the C++ library of MSVC makes `std::exception` the class `stdext::exception`, which holds only a pointer to its message, and `std::runtime_error(const std::string&)` stores `c_str()` of its argument. The string is freed at the end of the throw expression, before the catch block runs, so the bridge copied freed memory. When the new Rust allocation got the same heap block, the check of `ptr::copy_nonoverlapping` in a debug build stopped the process; otherwise the error held what the freed block held at that time. A message of several MiB has pages of its own, which the heap returns to the system when it is freed, and reading it is an access violation.
- With the same value the catch clause of `cxx`, `catch (std::exception const&)`, names `stdext::exception`. The exceptions of the C++ runtime derive from the `std::exception` of the runtime: `std::bad_alloc` of `operator new`, and `std::out_of_range` and `std::length_error` of the out-of-line functions that the C++ library calls (`_Xout_of_range`, `_Xlength_error`). They passed the catch clause and ended the process at the `noexcept` bridge function.
- The crate keeps every define of the WebRTC library, `_HAS_EXCEPTIONS=0` among them, so that its C++ and `webrtc.lib` use one configuration of the C++ library. With the other value in the crate, the library classes that keep their names under both values (`runtime_error`, `system_error`, `bad_optional_access`, `bad_variant_access`) and the inline functions `_Throw_bad_array_new_length`, `_Throw_bad_optional_access`, `_Throw_bad_variant_access` and `_Throw_tree_length_error` have a different layout or body in the crate and in `webrtc.lib`, and the linker keeps one copy of each name for the whole program. The runtime part of `cxx` (`cxx.cc`) is built by the build script of `cxx` without the WebRTC defines, as in the published crate.
- Every throw of the crate throws `livekit_ffi::Exception` (`include/livekit/exception.h`, new). The class derives from `std::exception`, keeps the message in a `std::string` member and returns it from `what()`, so the message exists until the exception has been destroyed after the catch block, with either value and on every system.
- The same header defines `rust::behavior::trycatch`, which `cxx` uses in place of its own catch in every bridge that includes the header. Each bridge of the crate with a C++ function that returns `Result` includes it. Only a `livekit_ffi::Exception` becomes a Rust error, with its message. The crate throws it in its own code, after the WebRTC call has returned, so no frame of the WebRTC library lies between the throw and the catch.
- Any other exception ends the process, on every system. It can have passed frames of the WebRTC library, which is built without exception handling: their destructors did not run and their locks were not released, so the program would continue on that state. The catch writes one line to standard error and calls `std::abort()`. The line names the bridge function (the type name of its function object, which holds the name of the generated function), the type of the exception and, for a `std::exception`, its `what()`. On macOS and Linux the type comes from the C++ ABI (`abi::__cxa_current_exception_type`). On Windows only the type of a `stdext::exception` is known; the line states "not known" for any other value, the exceptions of the C++ runtime among them. On Windows the catch first, before any other step, turns off the message box and the fault report of `abort()` (`_set_abort_behavior`). The first line needs no allocation and no lock of another thread: the process can end because its memory is exhausted, or while another thread holds a lock that a write would need, and either would stop the catch before the line (`std::bad_alloc` inside the `noexcept` catch ends the process through `std::terminate`; a held lock blocks for ever). The catch passes the `type_info` object of the bridge function and a pointer to the `std::exception`, and runs no code of the exception itself: the function reads the type and `what()` only after it has turned off the message box and set a terminate handler that only calls `std::abort()`, because a `what()` that allocates or throws would otherwise end the process through `std::terminate` before either. It reads no name with `type_info::name()` for the first line, because that function of MSVC undecorates the name into memory that it allocates; the first line has the names as the compiler wrote them, from `raw_name()` on Windows (the decorated name, such as `.?AV...`) and `name()` on macOS and Linux (on those systems the type of `int` is `i`). The line is formatted in a buffer on the stack and written to the handle of standard error, with `write` on macOS and Linux and `WriteFile` on Windows: `fprintf` and `fflush` take the lock of the stream `stderr` of the C library, which another thread can hold. A second line follows with the readable names, from `abi::__cxa_demangle` on macOS and Linux and `name()` on Windows. Both need memory, and the demangler of libc++abi does not return null when an allocation fails: its allocator calls `std::terminate()` and its output buffer `std::abort()`. Demangling before the line, as an earlier version did, ended a process with exhausted memory without any line; after the line, it can only lose the second line. The terminate handler that only aborts keeps such a failure from waiting: the default handler of libc++abi writes through the stream `stderr`, whose lock another thread can hold.
- Before, on Windows such an exception passed the catch clause of `cxx` and ended the process at the `noexcept` bridge function without a line; on macOS the catch clause of `cxx` returned it to Rust as an error. On Linux the crate is built against the libc++ inside `libwebrtc.a`, which is built without exceptions: where another C++ library throws, it ends the process itself (`out_of_range was thrown in -fno-exceptions mode`).
- Tests: `engine::tests::the_error_of_a_native_call_keeps_its_message_after_the_call_has_ended` parses a session description whose first line has 8 MiB and checks that the error holds that line. With the published throws it ended with an access violation on Windows in 20 of 20 runs. `engine::tests::a_native_peer_that_fails_initialization_can_release_its_wrapper` checks the message of the error of an invalid ICE URL; with the published throws it failed on Windows in 4 of 20 runs: twice the debug check stopped the process, and twice the message was the log line that the destructor of the peer wrote while the exception was thrown, in the freed block. Both are errors of the crate that reach Rust. `engine::tests::a_cpp_exception_that_the_wrapper_did_not_throw_ends_the_process_with_a_line_that_names_it` starts the test program again for each test function of the new bridge `src/exception.rs` (`std::vector::at` on an empty vector, not on Linux, and `throw 1`) and checks that the process ends (on macOS and Linux with `SIGABRT`) with a line that names the function and the type. With the catch of `cxx` it failed on macOS. It checks the second line. On every system it runs `throw 1` while another thread holds the lock of the stream `stderr` (`flockfile`, `_lock_file` on Windows), a test function of the bridge; with `fprintf` the process waited for the lock until the time limit. It throws a subclass of `std::exception` of the bridge (`throw_test_error`) and requires its type and its message in the first line, on Windows the decorated name `.?AVTestError@livekit_ffi@@`. On Windows it requires the decorated name in the first line of every case; the earlier catch printed the name of `name()`. A Windows test with exhausted memory would need a job object with a memory limit around the child process. On Linux it also runs `throw 1` in a process whose address space `ulimit -v` limits, after the process has taken every block that the allocator still gives, and requires the first line; with the earlier catch, which copied the names into `std::string` values, and with a version that demangled before the line, that process ended with `SIGABRT` and an empty standard error (Linux CI). Each process of the test has a time limit of 60 seconds, after which it is killed and the test fails, so that a message box of Windows or a stopped fatal path fails the test and does not stop it. `crates/media/tests/native_exceptions.rs` reads the sources: every throw of the crate throws `Exception` (the NVIDIA code, built only on Linux, and the test functions are excluded), and every bridge with a C++ function that returns `Result` includes `livekit/exception.h`. Both failed with the published sources. CI runs the tests on the three systems (ADR 0905).
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version throws only exceptions that own their message, also with `_HAS_EXCEPTIONS=0`, and its bridges end the process at every other C++ exception.

Publisher negotiation:

- With media server v1.13.9 the SDK connects in its single peer connection mode. A track that another participant publishes while this device is in the room reaches this device only through a new offer of this device: the media server asks for one, and its answer names the track of each media section. The device's own publications use the same offers. A negotiation request that is lost leaves every later remote track and every later publication of the connection without media, while the connection, the devices and the other tracks stay as they were.
- In the fast publish mode the SDK negotiates in one task at a time. A request while the task negotiates makes it negotiate once more after the current answer. In the published version the task set its state to idle and cleared a separate running flag in a later step. A request between those two steps found the state idle and the flag set, marked a retry and started no task; the task then ended. From then on every request found the retry marked and was dropped. In the window live test this left one person unheard for the rest of the call.
- The vendored copy decides with one state under one lock: idle, a task negotiates, or a task negotiates and then negotiates again. A task ends only after it has set the state to idle under that lock, so a request that comes later starts a new task. A request while a task waits for an answer is decided by the same state.
- The decisions are in their own file, which uses the standard library only. `crates/media/tests/negotiation_queue.rs` compiles that file and runs its tests in the workspace suite: a request between the last decision of a task and its end starts a new task, and requests from eight threads leave the queue idle. Both failed with the published decisions. The tests run outside an async runtime, so each runs on a thread of its own and fails when it has not ended within a time limit of the standard library.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version decides the queue under one state. Its provenance, the changed files and the retained licence notices are kept beside the dependency.

Order of mute requests:

- A mute or an unmute of a local track sends a request to the media server, which forwards nothing of a muted track. The SDK sends each request from a task of its own, and the task first waits until a restoration of the connection has ended. One change gives two requests: `LocalTrackPublication::mute` and `unmute` call the handler of the change through the track and then directly. In the published version each request started a new task, so each change started two. Tasks that waited together for one restoration were released together and sent in the order in which the threads of the runtime ran them. A mute that reached the media server after the unmute that followed it left the track muted at the server while the device sent and showed it open: the others heard nothing for the rest of the connection. The media live test `devices::the_chosen_devices_are_opened_and_a_missing_one_gives_way_to_the_default` failed this way at its last step, when a restoration spanned the close of the setting and the open that followed it.
- The vendored copy sends the mute requests of the local tracks of a participant one after the other. Each request starts on its own task, as before, and sends only after the request that was given before it has ended, or its task has ended without finishing. A request that waits for a restoration therefore holds back the requests after it, and the media server receives them in the order of the changes. The two requests of one change are both given before the next change, so the last request is the last change.
- The ordering is in its own file, which uses the standard library and Tokio only. `crates/media/tests/mute_order.rs` compiles that file and runs its tests in the workspace suite: an unmute does not overtake a mute that waits, 200 requests that each wait a different time arrive in their order on four threads, and a request whose task panics does not hold back the next. The first two failed with a task for each request, as in the published version. Each waits with a time limit, so a request that is held back forever fails the test.
- The participant needs a connected engine to run, so `crates/media/tests/mute_order.rs` also reads its source, without comments: the participant starts no task of its own and names one `InOrder`, the field that holds it; each mute request (`mute_track`) is given inside a `run` of that `InOrder`; the handlers of a mute and of an unmute each send one request. These checks fail with a task for each request, as in the published version, and with a new `InOrder` for each request. The media live test below can pass with a task for each request, because the order in which the runtime runs tasks that are released together can be the order of the changes.
- The media live test `sound::mute_requests_that_wait_for_a_resume_reach_the_media_server_in_their_order` checks the handlers of the participant with a connected engine. A TCP proxy between the clients and the signal port of the media server holds the open connections, and holds the answers of the media server to the connections that the clients open to restore them, so that the session of the device waits for its resume. The person changes the microphone seven times in that time, each change taken by the call library before the next. The proxy then passes the answers and records every request that the clients write. The requests for the track of the device must be the two requests of each change, in the order of the changes, and then one request with the state after the changes, which the participant sends after the resume; the media server must hold the last change. The test fails when no request follows the resume.
- The media live test `sound::a_mute_and_an_open_during_a_restored_connection_reach_the_media_server_in_order` pauses the media server until the clients restore their connection, mutes and opens the microphone three times 200 ms apart, and checks that the other person hears the device and that the server does not hold its track as muted. It failed in 2 of 8 runs with the published version.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version sends the mute requests of a track in their order. The copy is removed when no correction remains.

Mute state after a resume:

- The SDK counts a mute request as sent when it has written it to the signal connection. A connection that is lost without a close is found only when its pings are not answered, up to the ping timeout later. A request written in that time stays with the network and reaches the media server late or not at all. When the SDK resumes the session on a new signal connection, the media server (v1.13.9) closes the request channel of the old connection before it answers the resume, and discards a request that arrives on the old connection later. The resume (`SyncState`) sends the publications with the track information of the `AddTrack` response, whose mute flag is the state at the time of publishing: the SDK does not update that information of a local publication later. The media server does not take the mute state from the resume. In the published version an open written in that time was therefore lost: the media server held the track as muted for the rest of the connection while the device sent and showed it open.
- The vendored copy sends the mute state of each local track again after each resume, in the same order as the other mute requests of the participant. Each such request reads the state when the requests given before it have ended, and a later change gives a request after it, so the last request holds the current state. A full reconnect publishes each track again with its mute state and needs no such request.
- The media live test `sound::an_open_written_to_a_lost_signal_connection_reaches_the_media_server_after_the_resume` puts a TCP proxy between the clients and the signal port of the media server. The proxy holds the open connections in both directions, the test opens the microphone and waits until the client has resumed through a new connection, and then the proxy delivers what the client wrote on the old connection. The test decodes the signal requests that the client wrote to the held connection and checks that the open is among them: a client that had found the loss before the open would keep the request and send it on the new connection, and the test would then pass without the correction. This check failed when the test opened the microphone after the client showed the interruption. The other person must hear the device, and the media server must hold the track as open. It failed in 3 of 3 runs without the request after the resume and passed in 8 of 8 with it.
- The media live test of the order of the mute requests requires one request with the state after the changes after the requests of the changes that waited for the resume. `crates/media/tests/mute_order.rs` reads the source of the room, the local participant and the participant: the room has the local participant send the mute states in `handle_resumed`, and the request of each local track reads the state (`is_muted`) inside the `run` of the `InOrder` and sends it. Where `send_mute_states` reads the state is not shown by the live test: the state of a publication changes before the request of the change is given, so a state that is read when the request is given is the newest one as well, and a later change gives a request after it.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version sends the current mute state after a resume. It is also removed when a pinned upstream version updates the publication information that the resume sends at each mute change and the pinned media server takes the mute state from the resume. A media server that took the mute state from the resume of the vendored version would take the state at the time of publishing: a microphone that was published muted and opened later would be muted again at the server at each resume.

Negotiation after a close:

- The close of a room unpublishes each local track, and each unpublish asks for a negotiation of the publisher; then the room closes the session and its peer connections. The published version started these negotiations while the close went on. In the fast publish mode a negotiation that waited for an answer of a closed session waited 10 seconds, one that made its offer after the close logged the warning "peer connection is closed, cannot create offer" and then waited, and one whose local description came after the close logged the error "Failed to set local offer sdp: Called in wrong state: closed".
- The vendored copy stops the negotiations before the tracks are unpublished: `RoomSession::close` calls `RtcEngine::begin_close` first, and from then on the session starts no negotiation of the publisher (`publisher_negotiation_needed` returns with a debug line). `begin_close` and the close of the session end a wait for an answer. The engine leaves the unpublishing of its own tracks to that close: a leave and a reset of the connection take the room from the engine before they end the camera, the share and the microphone (`close_connection`), so their withdrawals unpublish nothing, and the close of the room unpublishes them after `begin_close`. `offer_publisher`, the one function that makes an offer in both modes, makes none in a session that closes, and takes an offer whose result comes after the close began as the end of the negotiation, with a debug line; a failure of an open session is still an error.
- The media live test `close::a_closing_room_starts_no_negotiation_and_ends_the_wait_for_an_answer` is a test program of its own (`crates/media/tests/close`), holds the media server alone and has one person in the room, so that every line about a negotiation that it reads is hers: another person's publisher negotiates when she leaves, the lines do not name their session, and a task of another test that its runtime cancelled would leave a start without an end. A layer of the log subscriber of the call tests notes these lines with their time, and the test reads them from its own start. Five times it waits until her microphone and camera send and no negotiation runs, leaves the call, and fails when the leave started a negotiation of the publisher. Then it pauses the media server, stops her camera, which negotiates the publisher, and leaves once the offer is sent: it fails unless that negotiation ends within half of the 10 seconds for which the SDK waits for an answer. It also fails when the call library logged a failed negotiation or an offer in a closed connection.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version does not negotiate the publisher of a room that closes.

Call diagnostics:

- Each media engine has a process-local numeric log identifier. Logs name connection attempts, room connection and closure, sound-state changes, local publication and remote subscription changes and failures. They never log join tokens.
- At the `info` log level, an engine with a room reports sound progress every five seconds: connection age, controls, publication and mute state, remote subscriptions, the capture gate, recent frame counts for each sound stage, dropped capture frames and playback queues. Existing counters are used; no audio samples are logged. The default desktop log level remains `warn`.
- These reports distinguish an unopened microphone, a closed gate, missing publication or subscription, absent incoming frames and playback starvation. They are available in the product as well as in tests, including when a failed wait does not explicitly request a sound report.

TLS trust:

- The pinned WebRTC binary (`webrtc-89d790b`) verifies TLS peers against its compiled-in roots first. Its platform verifier is a fallback: macOS uses an SSL server policy with `SecTrust`; Windows uses the system certificate chain engine and SSL server policy. Linux has no platform verifier in that binary, and its compiled-in roots omit the ISRG roots. A valid TURN TLS certificate can therefore fail on Linux even when the system trusts its issuer.
- The vendored wrapper registers a Linux platform verifier during native runtime initialization, before starting its network threads. Every default TLS adapter then has this fallback, including TURN. `rustls-native-certs` reads the system's certificate files and directories, including the standard `SSL_CERT_FILE` and `SSL_CERT_DIR` overrides. The existing Rustls certificate verifier validates the complete peer chain, its signatures, validity dates, constraints and SSL server purpose against those roots. Roots are loaded for each chain verification so new handshakes see changes to the trust files. An absent usable root or an invalid chain fails verification. The Linux fallback does not fetch revocation information; it uses the installed CA files and the current time.
- On all three systems, a peer is trusted if the compiled-in roots **or** the system trust store accept its chain. The unchanged `SSLPostConnectionCheck` still verifies the hostname. A root distrusted by the system can remain trusted through the compiled-in set. A system-only policy would require rebuilding WebRTC with its built-in roots excluded; this patch does not change that binary or its revision.
- The Linux correction is removed when a pinned upstream version provides system-root verification for every default TLS adapter. The standalone test participant selects the same vendored wrapper as the workspace so deployment checks exercise the correction. Its regression uses the actual peer connection against a loopback TLS server and requires a TURN Allocate request after successful verification. A temporary system-CA file is selected only for the test child process; no host trust store is changed. Unknown issuers, mismatched hostnames and certificates restricted to client use must not receive an Allocate request. Windows still requires its native TLS trust checks in CI.

Extraction of the WebRTC archive:

- The build script of `webrtc-sys` calls `webrtc-sys-build` 0.3.19 to download the WebRTC archive of its target and extract it with `ZipArchive::extract` of the `zip` crate. The published `webrtc-sys-build` 0.3.19 depends on `zip` 0.6.6. Its `extract` keeps the setuid, setgid and sticky bits of an entry (RUSTSEC-2026-0367) and applies the permissions of an entry through an extracted symbolic link without checking that the link target is inside the destination (RUSTSEC-2026-0369). `zip` 9.0.0 corrects both. RUSTSEC-2026-0368 describes a fault of `zip` 9.0.0-pre2 that 9.0.0 also corrects; it names no unaffected earlier version, so `cargo deny` reports it against 0.6.6 as well. No published version of `webrtc-sys-build` depends on a corrected `zip`, and the upstream repository still depends on `zip` 0.6.
- The workspace and the standalone test participant use the build inputs of `webrtc-sys-build` 0.3.19 from `vendor/`. Its manifest depends on `zip` 9.0.4 with the feature `deflate-flate2-zlib-rs` only: the crate reads archives and writes none, and the feature `deflate` of `zip` 9 also adds the zopfli compressor. The source code, the download address and the WebRTC binary revision are unchanged.
- The advisories are not ignored in `deny.toml`. The build reads an archive from the upstream release page over TLS; a changed or replaced archive at that address would otherwise create files with these permissions on the build machine.
- Checks: `cargo deny check` passes, and a build from an empty build directory downloads and extracts the archive with `zip` 9. The extracted tree of `mac-arm64-release` was compared with the one that `zip` 0.6.6 made: the same paths, file types, permissions and contents. `junto-media` builds and links on macOS and in the Linux container, and the media live tier passes.
- This correction is removed when a pinned upstream version of `webrtc-sys-build` depends on a corrected `zip`.

TLS implementation references: [platform selection](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/89d790b/rtc_base/BUILD.gn), [platform verifier registration](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/89d790b/rtc_base/platform_certificate_verifier.h), [verification and hostname checks](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/89d790b/rtc_base/openssl_adapter.cc).

## Consequences

- The client has a volume per person and a level per person, and the tests carry a tone through the same code as a voice of the product: only the device differs.
- The project owns the pipeline between the devices and the SDK: the conversion, the mix, the queues and the use of the processing module. An error there is an error of the project.
- The echo canceller gets its reference from the mix that the client plays. It estimates the delay between the mix and the microphone by itself; no delay is given to it.
- New dependencies: `cpal` (Apache-2.0) for the devices, and on macOS `objc2-av-foundation`, `objc2-foundation`, `objc2` and `block2` for the microphone permission. `cargo deny check` passes with them.
- The platform bindings contain the unsafe calls: AVFoundation and ScreenCaptureKit on macOS, Windows capture including Media Foundation cameras, and the PipeWire initialisation and V4L2 camera bindings on Linux. On every system the scale of received planes through the vendored `webrtc-sys` (`share::picture::scale`) has one unsafe call. Camera plane validation, colour conversion and the decisions of V4L2 capture (the choice of a mode, the handling of a returned buffer, the matrix of an encoding, ADR 0770) remain safe Rust. The device checks have their own unsafe modules: the virtual camera source of Windows, a COM server, and the registration and start of virtual cameras in `camera-device` (ADR 0913); the bindings of the V4L2 device in memory are part of the tests of the Linux camera module. The selection of a Linux sound host and its device snapshot remain safe Rust (ADRs 0736 and 0750).
- A muted microphone stays open, so the system shows its microphone indicator while a person is muted in a call. Unmute has no delay.
- The output follows a change of the default device of the system only as far as `cpal` does. A person chooses a device in the call when the sound does not follow.
- At 22.05 kHz and 11.025 kHz the frames of a microphone are made two and four at a time, which adds up to 10 ms and 30 ms of delay.
- A device with more than two input channels gives its first two as the microphone. A device with more than two output channels plays the mix on its first two.
- A future of `Calls` that reads from a device costs one task of the runtime of the client. The device interfaces are not an interface for the window: a future of one of them that the window polled would fail on macOS and on Linux.
- A build of the desktop client downloads and links the WebRTC library. The build directory grows by about 1 GiB for it.
