# 0761. The administration in the window

## Status

Accepted

## Context

A person with the permissions manages workspaces, categories, channels, overrides, members, invites, join requests, access tokens and exports, and reads the audit log (ADR 0903, step 5). The approved design has no view for any of these. ADR 0703 fixes three columns, one job per area, and the components of ADR 0731. The client core holds the collections of the administration on demand (ADR 0760). The server decides every request, takes no reason for a removal or a ban, has no override for a single member (ADR 0301), and filters the audit log by a page position only (ADR 0305). The screen that creates, edits, orders and assigns roles waits for the role order of the server and is not part of this record.

## Decision

One layer:

- The administration is one layer above the window: a list of pages at the left and one page at the right. The three columns of the window stay as the design has them. The layer holds the keyboard focus, and Escape, its close button and a click outside close it.
- The menu of the server opens it. A button in the header of a channel opens the same layer with the settings of that channel.
- The pages of a server are in three groups. Server: Workspaces, People, Invites, Join requests, Access tokens, Server roles, Audit log. The shown workspace: Channels, Roles, Archive and retention, Members, Recordings. The account: Exports. ADR 0791 decides the two pages of the roles, and ADR 0784 the page of the recordings. The settings of a channel have Overview, Permissions, and Archive and deletion.
- A page that the person may not use is absent. On a page that the person sees, a control that the person may not use is disabled, with the reason as a sentence or a tooltip. What is possible follows the server permissions of the account and the lists of workspace permissions that the server states for the workspace and for a channel (ADR 0790). The server decides each request.
- A page that is asked for and that the person may not use gives way to another one: in the administration the page of the workspaces, in the settings of a channel the page Archive and deletion, which the gear opens for a person without "manage channel". The page that is shown becomes the selected page. A permission that arrives later adds its pages to the list and does not replace the page that is shown, because a press that started on that page would end on another one (ADR 0730). Tests: `admin_pages::a_role_that_arrives_keeps_the_shown_page_of_the_channel_settings` and `admin_pages::a_permission_that_returns_keeps_the_shown_page_of_the_administration`.
- A form and a question are drawn in the row that they belong to, in place of the actions of that row. No second layer opens above the first. A deletion, an archiving, a removal and a ban are questions that state what follows. When the answer closes a form or a question, or a change of the state puts it away, its row keeps the height that it had with it, so the controls under the row do not move. The row keeps that height until the person opens or puts away a form or question of the page, the page changes or the layer closes; nothing else on the page ends it (ADR 0730). The code of a new invite and the secret of a new access token are drawn in the row of their form, in the place of the form. The row of the new invite or token joins its list when the person puts the code or secret away, or when the space of the row is released in another way, and a row that the state brings later waits for the next release (ADR 0730). While the request of a new invite or token is sent, its form cannot be put away and the actions of the rows of its list are disabled, so that the answer finds its form; the page change and the closing of the layer drop the answer (ADR 0730).
- Each request has the states sending and refused. A refusal is a sentence at the control that asked, chosen by the error code of the server. A request that the server did leaves no state of its own: the change arrives with the state of the server. Only the answer to the newest request of the open page changes what the page tells and holds. An answer to an earlier request, and an answer that arrives after the page changed or the layer closed, is dropped with what it carries, a code or a token included; the change that its request made arrives with the state of the server.
- A form whose request is on its way is not sent a second time, by its button or by Enter.
- The layer belongs to the account that its server showed when it opened. When that server shows another account, or none, the layer closes and keeps nothing of the account before.
- The window holds open the collections that the open page shows and releases them when the page changes. A collection that is on its way or was not read is stated in place of its rows, with "Try again" after a failure.

The lists of a page:

- Audit entries, recordings, accounts, members and addable accounts, invites, join requests, access tokens, exports, workspaces, categories and channels, archived channels, the roles of the permissions of a channel and the roles of a server or workspace are drawn by the list of gpui, whose rows have their own heights. The header and the footer of the page are rows of the list. The list lays out the rows in view and the rows within 800 pixels before and after them. With 2,000 audit entries a frame lays out fewer than 100 rows. The list is one component (`shell::admin::list`) that the window and its unit tests draw.
- The page gives each row a key from what the row shows, such as the identifier of an entry. When the rows of a page change, the rows that keep their order keep their focus handle and their measured height, and each run of removed, added or moved rows between them is replaced on its own. The first row in view that keeps its place stays at the same place in the view. A row that moves from above the view, such as an account that another person renames, therefore moves no row in view. A row that holds the keyboard focus and moves, such as a channel that the keyboard moves, keeps its focus handle, so its control keeps the keys, and the list scrolls it into view.
- A row that was not laid out has an estimated height of 40 pixels of the design at the scale of the window. The scroll bar uses it until the row is laid out. gpui forgets every height when the width of the list changes; the list then gives the estimate again. After a change of the scale it measures every row again.
- The page states for each row whether the row can hold a control that takes the keyboard focus. It derives this from the data that builds the controls of the row: "Open" and "Settings" of an archived channel take the focus whatever the permission, and an audit entry has a control only when it names a deleted workspace.
- Tab from the last control of a row and Shift+Tab from its first control go to the next or the previous row that can hold a control, also when that row is not drawn. The list scrolls the row into view, and moves the focus when a frame has drawn the row in the view: before that, the row is not in the tab order of the window. The focus stays on the control where the key was pressed until then. The move ends without a change of the focus when the focus changed in the meantime, and after 8 frames in which the row was not drawn in the view; the row is scrolled into view again in each of them, because its estimated height can place it outside the view. A row that holds no control that takes the focus at that time, such as when all of its controls are disabled, passes the move on to the next row, or out of the list. From the first and the last control of the list, the focus leaves the list as from any control of the layer, which keeps it inside the layer.
- While the focus is outside the rows, the list draws an empty tab stop before its rows and one after them. When no row in view holds a control, Tab and Shift+Tab reach these tab stops, and the list moves the focus on to the first or the last row that can hold a control.
- Page Up, Page Down, Home and End scroll the list from any element in it. Down and Up on a focused row, such as an audit entry that assistive technology focused, move the focus to the next and the previous row and scroll it into view. Each row that is an item of the list, not a header, a footer or the header of a group, has the role of a list item, its position and the count of the items. A row that is not laid out is not in the accessibility tree.
- A step of a wheel that counts lines scrolls the list by the line height of the list, as the other scrolled elements of the window do. gpui scrolls its list by 20 pixels a line.
- A form keeps its height in the state of the page whether its row is drawn or not (ADR 0730). A release of that height measures every row again.
- Tests. The unit tests of `shell::admin::list` draw the list in a window of the test platform without a server: a row moved from above the view, a focused row moved out of the view, Tab into a list whose rows in view hold no control, Tab to a row far away, a Tab whose row was not drawn before the person moved the focus, the estimate after the first frame and after a change of the width and of the rem size, Up and Down, an action that reaches a row of the last frame after the list was replaced, and a wheel step in lines. The window tests load 2,000 audit entries and 2,000 accounts and check that fewer than 100 rows are laid out, the keyboard path through the audit log, the archived channels of a member without "manage channel", the scroll extent after a change of the width of the window, and the place of the rows in view when a member renames itself. The tests of a form answer bring the control that sends the form and the row under it into view together before the first measurement, so that the press on the control scrolls nothing, and require the row of the form and the row under it in view at both measurements.
- `JUNTO_SCREENS_ONLY=audit-frames` measures frames of the audit log with 50 and 2,000 entries through the real window renderer: without a change, after a wheel scroll, after a jump of the scroll bar to one of 101 places, and after a newer entry arrives at the top. Each case discards five frames and reports the median and the longest of 60. The test `admin_pages::more_than_two_thousand_audit_entries_are_read_page_by_page_once_each_in_order` reads 2,060 entries of the server page by page, to measure the cost of the pages apart from the cost of drawing them.

In a release headless run on macOS, 2,000 entries took a median 220.62 ms per unchanged frame and 221.96 ms per scrolled frame before the change, and 4.13 ms and 4.08 ms after it. The longest measured frames changed from 250.85 ms and 276.69 ms to 5.58 ms and 4.88 ms. With 50 entries, the medians changed from 9.19 ms and 8.64 ms to 2.87 ms and 2.83 ms. After the change, a jump of the scroll bar takes a median 5.11 ms (longest 6.47 ms) with 2,000 entries and 3.09 ms with 50, and a newer entry 7.83 ms (longest 9.34 ms) with 2,000 entries and 3.22 ms with 50. The figures before the change come from `crates/client/src/bin/junto-screens/audit-frames-baseline.patch` applied to commit `777578e`, the last commit that drew every row; it adds the unchanged and the scrolled cases of the same measurement there. These are measurements of the fixed screen fixture, not a limit promised for every server or machine.

Pages:

- Workspaces: the list with the default workspace and the membership of the account, a new workspace, a new name, the deletion, and an invite code that enters further workspaces (ADR 0115).
- Channels: the categories and the channels of the shown workspace in the order of the channel list; a new category and a new channel in a category; a new name, the order, the move of a channel into a category, and the deletion of a category. A move by one place is the move to the place before or after, with the rule of positions below.
- Channels, drag (ADR 0703): a channel that the person manages has a handle at the start of its row. A drop on the row of a channel puts the dragged channel at that place of that group, and the channels from that place on move one place down; a drop on the heading of a category, or on the heading of the channels without a category, puts it at the end of that group.
- Channels, positions: the state and the server order the channels of a group by position and then by identifier, and two channels can share a position. The page changes the fewest channels. The moved channel takes a position next to one of its new neighbours: the position of the channel before it or one above, or the position of the channel after it or one below; in an empty group, 0. A channel after it whose order would no longer follow moves up to the lowest position at which it follows the channel before it: that channel's position when its identifier comes after, else the one above; and so on until the order follows. A channel before it moves down in the same way. A position can be below 0 (the route takes any 32-bit position), so the first and the last place need one changed channel, and a way that would pass the bounds of a 32-bit position is not taken. Of two ways with as many changes, the one that gives the moved channel a position of its own comes first, and then the one that moves the channels after it. Tests: `structure::tests::an_entry_moved_inside_its_group_changes_the_fewest_entries`, `an_entry_shares_a_position_when_the_identifiers_keep_the_order` (201 channels at position 0), `a_moved_entry_keeps_a_shared_position_when_the_identifiers_allow_it`, `without_a_free_place_the_entries_move_to_the_next_free_position`, `a_move_at_the_bounds_of_a_position_changes_the_fewest_entries`, `view::tests::a_move_takes_the_way_that_changes_only_channels_that_the_person_manages`.
- Channels, moves: a drop, Up, Down and a place of "Move to" compute their moves from the page as it is drawn, the same page that decided that the control is offered, and send them in one request of the route of ADR 0304. Each move states the category and the position where the page read the channel. The channels next to the place, as the page read them, follow as moves whose new place is their place: the server only checks that they are there, which needs "view channel" alone. The server applies all of them or none, and refuses with `conflict` when a channel is no longer there and with `forbidden` when the person may no longer change one of them. A page that is older than the server therefore never moves part of a group, and never puts a channel between two channels that another person moved apart. A place takes the drop only when one of the ways changes only channels in which the person holds "manage channel" and, with the checks, names at most 200 channels; of the ways that qualify the one with the fewest changes is sent.
- Channels, more than 200 moves: a move that names more than 200 channels in every way is not offered. Up and Down are disabled, and a place of "Move to" is disabled, each with the reason that the move changes the places of more than 200 channels and one change takes 200 at most; a drop there is not taken. This happens only when the place lies inside a run of channels with consecutive positions whose identifiers order the moved channel beside neither neighbour, with about 200 channels of the run on each side of it. Reason: the window does not split a move into several requests, because a failure between them would leave a part of the group moved, which the route of ADR 0304 exists to prevent; and the route keeps its bound, because each changed channel writes an event and an entry of the audit log in the one transaction. The bound is the one constant `MOST_CHANNEL_MOVES` of the protocol, which the route and the window read. Test: `view::tests::a_move_names_at_most_the_channels_that_one_request_names`.
- Channels, a sent move: the page draws the moved channels at the places of the move from the send until the state holds the events of its answer (ADR 0214), and states those places as the places where the channels are, so a further move starts from them. The order therefore changes at the press or the drop of the person, and the answer and the events move no row and no control (ADR 0730). The form of "Move to" stays in the row of the moved channel, at its new place, until the answer, and the row keeps its height after the answer as every row with a form does. When the state holds a move, the moves of the same channels in earlier requests are no longer drawn, so a late answer of an earlier request does not draw an older place over the state. Tests: `admin::a_move_changes_the_order_at_the_press_and_no_row_moves_at_its_answer` holds the request of a move at a proxy, and compares the bounds of the controls of the other channels after the press and after the answer and the events; `moves::tests::a_later_move_that_the_state_holds_takes_the_same_channel_out_of_earlier_moves`.
- Channels, a refused move: a move that the server refuses stays drawn where it is, with the refusal in the row of the moved channel, on one line in place of its actions with the full text as its tooltip, and a Close button, until the person acts again on the layer: Close, a form or a question that opens or is put away, another page, or the closing of the layer. While it is drawn the page offers no move, with the reason that a refused move is shown. The channels that the request only checked stay where the page drew them too. The rows therefore do not move at the refusal, which arrives at a time that the person does not choose (ADR 0730); they return to the order of the state at the act of the person. A refusal of "Move to" closes its form, and the row keeps the height of the form (the rule of ADR 0730 for a form that its answer closes); the form stays drawn until the answer. A refusal with `conflict` says that the page read an order that the server no longer holds: the state is read again at once, and the page shows it at Close. A refusal whose channel the page no longer lists, such as a channel deleted meanwhile, is stated at the end of the page with its Close button, where it moves no row above it. Tests: `admin::a_refused_move_to_keeps_the_height_of_its_form` (a form whose places take several lines), `admin::a_refused_move_of_a_channel_that_is_gone_is_stated_on_the_page`. Test: `admin::a_move_from_an_old_order_is_refused_and_stays_drawn_until_the_person_acts` moves a neighbour on the server while the events wait at a proxy, compares the bounds of the rows after the press and after the refusal, waits for the state read again with the events still held, and finds the new place of the neighbour at Close.
- Channels, keyboard (ADR 0702): "Move to" on the page of the channels has two steps in the row: the group, and then every place of it, "At the start" and "After" each channel. A place is enabled when a drop there is taken, so the keyboard reaches every destination of a drag, also a place between two channels that the person may not change. Test: `admin::the_keyboard_reaches_every_place_that_a_drag_reaches`. The "Move to" of the overview of a channel changes its category only. The drag is on this page and not in the channel column: the column groups the channels without a category by kind (ADR 0730), and their positions are one order of the server for both kinds, so a place between two rows of the column states no place in that order. Test: `admin::an_administrator_drags_channels_to_another_place_and_category`.
- Channel settings, Overview: the name, the category, "permanent messages", "private channel", and for a voice channel the user limit and "hide who is inside". "Private channel" is the deny of "view channel" in the override of the "everyone" role (ADR 0304). The lock and meeting mode stay controls of a running call (ADR 0739). An archived channel shows its settings and takes no change.
- Channel settings, Permissions: per role of the workspace one choice per permission: allow, default, deny. A choice that the grant rule of ADR 0304 refuses is disabled with its reason: to allow a permission, or to lift a deny of it, the person must hold it in the channel. Overrides exist per role only.
- Channel settings, Archive and deletion: archive or restore, the export, and the deletion. The deletion of a channel with permanent messages is offered to the server owner only (ADR 0402).
- Archive and retention: the archived channels of the workspace (ADR 0762), and for an administrator the archive period, the retention of files and of recordings of the workspace, and the retention of the files of direct messages.
- Server, for an administrator: the logo (ADR 0799) and the limits of the files of messages (ADR 0408), the largest file and the files per message. Each limit is a row with "Change", which opens a form with one field in the row: the largest file in whole megabytes from 1 to 1,073, because the server takes at most 1,073,741,824 bytes, and the files from 1 to 100. A number outside the range is not sent, and the form states the range. The route takes both limits in one request, so the form sends the other limit as the page shows it; a change of the other limit by another person in the same moment is replaced. The page reads the limits as a collection (ADR 0760), and the new limits arrive with their event. Test: `logo::an_administrator_changes_the_limits_of_files_and_the_window_of_a_member_follows`.
- People: the accounts of the server with their status and the names of their server roles; removal, ban and unban with a question; a filter for the banned accounts; for the server owner, the recovery of an account onto a new device (below). Members: the members of the shown workspace with the names of their roles, and for an administrator the accounts that can be added and the removal of a member from the workspace (ADR 0797). The roles of an account are given and taken in these two lists, and role order decides which account takes a removal or a ban (ADR 0791).
- Invites: the list with creator, expiry, uses, workspaces and state; a new invite with expiry, uses and the workspaces that the person may grant (ADR 0111); the revocation. Join requests: the list, the approval with workspaces, and the denial.
- Access tokens: the list, a new token with a name, actions and an expiry of at most 366 days (ADR 0210), and the revocation. An administrator has the page among the pages of the server, with the tokens of every account. Every other account has it among the pages of the account, with its own tokens and without the creation: the server lets an account revoke its own tokens after it lost the administrator permission.
- Exports: the exports of the account with state, size and expiry; the download into the download directory with its progress; the removal. An export is asked for in the settings of a channel and in the header of a direct conversation (ADR 0411).
- Audit log: the log of the server and the log of the shown workspace, read in pages of 50 with "Load older"; one readable line per action; an action that this build does not know is shown with its time and its actor (ADR 0209). The filters by action, by person, by the thing concerned and by period are applied by the server; past 2,000 entries the page goes on in place of the loaded entries; a row names the access token of its action; the log of a deleted workspace opens from the entry of its deletion (ADR 0797).

Recovery of an account (ADR 0104, ADR 0112):

- The row of every account has "Recover" for the server owner only, because the server lets only the owner recover (ADR 0104). For another person the action is not drawn. The own account of the owner and an account that is not active have the action disabled with its reason: the own account gets a new device by a link (ADR 0782), and the server recovers an active account only.
- "Recover" draws a form in the row: the question with the name of the account, a field for the key of the new device, an optional field for the name of the new device, and a sentence that the new device gets access to the account and its direct messages, that every session of the account ends, and that every device of the account is told.
- The key is the text that the new device shows while it waits for a link, in groups of four characters (ADR 0782). The spaces are removed before the key is read. A text that is no public key, and a device name that the rule of ADR 0112 refuses, are told at the form and not sent. Reason: the server would take any key of the right form, and a recovery cannot be undone from the window, so a typed key is checked before the request.
- The answer states when the server did the recovery. The row draws that in the place of the form with "Done", by the rule for a result that an answer carries (ADR 0730). Opening another form or question of the page puts it away too.
- The refusals are told at the row: the key belongs to an account already (`key_already_registered`), the account is not active (`conflict`), and the person is not the owner (`owner_only`).
- While the recovery is sent, the Cancel button of the form and the actions of every row of the page are disabled, with the reason that the server has not answered yet, as for the forms of ADR 0730: a release of the row before the answer would let the result move the rows under it, and a second request would hide the answer of the first. This includes the Roles control of a row and the switches of the roles of an account that are open under its row: the first puts the request of the page away, and a switch replaces it. Test: `recovery::the_owner_recovers_an_account_onto_a_new_device_and_the_account_lists_the_recovery` presses Cancel, Ban, Roles and an open role switch while the recovery waits at a proxy.
- Test: `recovery::the_owner_recovers_an_account_onto_a_new_device_and_the_account_lists_the_recovery` loses the only device of an account, asks for a link on a new installation, copies the key that it shows into the form of the owner after a refused text, and sees the new device log in by itself to the same account; while the proxy of the test holds the request, Cancel and the ban of another account change nothing. The test failed before the row had the action, and before Cancel was disabled while the request was sent.

Secrets:

- The code of a new invite and the token of a new access token are held by the open page only, shown once with "Copy", and gone from the state of the application when the person puts them away, changes the page or closes the layer. Neither is the accessibility label of its element. The copies that the drawing of a text makes are freed with the frame and are not erased, and a copy on the clipboard stays until the person replaces it.
- The file of an export is written beside its final name and renamed at the end, under a name that no file has. A body with more bytes than the export states is refused before the excess is written. A body that ends early is asked for again from the byte reached, with a wait before each further request, at most 5 times in a row without progress. Progress is 64 KiB beyond the furthest byte reached so far, or the end of the file, so a server that gives single bytes ends the save and an honest download of any size that is cut often ends whole. A save goes on when the layer closes. It is stopped, and its unfinished file removed, when its server leaves the client or shows another account.

## Consequences

- Every screen of the administration is designed in this record and not in the approved design.
- A person without any permission still has the layer: the workspaces, the invite code, the archived channels, the members, the own access tokens and the own exports.
- A removal and a ban take an optional reason, which the audit entry keeps (ADR 0797).
- A filter of the audit log is applied by the server, so it finds entries that the page has not loaded (ADR 0797).
- A move of channels is one request, applied whole or not at all. A move of a category sends two requests, or one request for each category when positions are equal; a failure between them leaves the order of the first requests.
- The roles are created, changed and ordered on the pages of ADR 0791.
- A person who holds "manage channel" through an override of one channel has the pages of the settings of that channel (ADR 0790).
