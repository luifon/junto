# 0304. Details of the permission function: view permission, grant rule and stored sets

## Status

Accepted

## Context

ADR 0301 fixes the two levels of roles, the evaluation order, the administrator permission and its limits. An implementation needs rules that it leaves open: which permission makes a channel visible, what exactly "nobody grants a permission they do not hold" covers, which permission the actions without a named permission need, which workspaces an invite can grant, and how a set of permissions is stored.

## Decision

One function:

- The crate `junto-authz` has one function that takes the caller, a request and the roles and overrides that the request needs, and returns allow or a reason for the denial. Every route calls it. A route contains no comparison of roles or permissions.
- The function runs in three steps: the limits, the administrator permission, the roles. A request that a limit decides never reaches the second step.
- The server owner passes every check that the administrator permission passes.

View permission:

- The workspace permission "view channel" is added to the list of ADR 0301. A private channel is a channel whose override of the "everyone" role denies it.
- Every permission in a channel needs "view channel" in that channel as well.
- An absent channel reads the same workspace-role, membership, person-rule and effective-channel-rule statements as a hidden channel before returning `not_found`. An absent category likewise runs the category and child scope reads before the visibility decision. Database-statistics tests compare the statements of repeated requests; they do not assert identical elapsed time. Only the `not_found` result waits for the remaining reads; a database error of a read is returned at once, because PostgreSQL refuses every later statement of a failed transaction and a serialization failure must reach the retry of ADR 0009.
- The "everyone" role of a new workspace has "view channel", "lock room" and "run meeting mode".

Membership:

- A request inside a workspace is denied to an account that is not a member of it, unless the account has the administrator permission.
- The person rules of an account (its workspace permissions and its rules in categories and channels) stay when the account leaves the workspace or is removed from it, and apply again when the account becomes a member again. Reason: a deny that ended with the membership would return access with no rule change and no audit entry. A rule of an account that is not a member is not evaluated, because the account gets nothing in the workspace. The rules end with the account, the workspace, the category or the channel, or when an administrator removes them. The rule routes accept the rule of an account that is not a member only to remove it (a request that sets or changes it answers `not_found`), and the lists of rules still show it; the client shows the person as no longer a member. Migration `0043_person_rules_survive_leaving` replaces the foreign keys of the person-rule tables to the workspace members by foreign keys to the account and to the workspace.

Grant rule:

- A change is checked against the permissions that it can add to any account:
  - creating a role: the permissions of the role;
  - editing a role: the permissions that the edit adds;
  - assigning a role: the permissions of the role, and for each channel the permissions that the channel overrides of the role allow there;
  - removing a role from a member, and deleting a role (ADR 0308): for each channel the permissions that the channel overrides of the role deny there, because the member gets them back;
  - changing a rule of a role or a person in a channel or a category: the permissions added to the allow set and the permissions removed from the deny set;
  - syncing a channel again, and moving a synced channel into another category: for each subject, what the new rules add compared with the rules that the channel uses now;
  - changing the workspace permissions of a member, and the server permissions of an account: ADR 0306.
- The caller must hold each of those permissions: in the workspace for the permissions of a role, in the channel for the permissions of an override. A caller who cannot see a channel holds nothing in it.
- Removing a permission from a role and adding a deny need only the managing permission. So does removing a role whose overrides deny nothing.
- The rule for the removal of a role is the same for every target: the caller's own account and another member.

Managing permissions:

- Workspace roles need "manage roles". Channels, categories and channel overrides need "manage channel".
- A channel at the root of the list, outside every category, needs "manage channel" in the workspace: to be created there and to be moved there from a category, by the route of the channel, the positions route or a structure file. Deleting a category that holds a channel moves its channels to the root, so it needs "manage channel" in the workspace as well; an empty category is deleted with "manage channel" in the category alone. A channel enters a category with "manage channel" in that category. A move inside the root or inside a category needs "manage channel" in the channel only. Reason: before, a person who managed only a category could move its channels to the root, where that person could not create a channel; the root is a place of the workspace, as a category is a place of its own rules. Test: `category_visibility::a_channel_enters_the_root_with_what_a_channel_created_there_needs`.
- Server roles, renaming a workspace and adding an existing account to a workspace (ADR 0115) need the administrator permission, because ADR 0301 names no other permission for them.
- Removal, ban and unban need "ban members". The server owner and the caller's own account cannot be the target (ADR 0113).
- Creating an invite or approving a join request needs its server permission, and the caller must be a member of every workspace that it grants.
- The account that creates a workspace becomes its first member and gets no role in it.

Attachment uploads:

- Starting an upload, adding a chunk and completing it require attach files and at least one of send messages or send messages in threads. The destination thread is not chosen until the message is sent. Each phase rechecks the current permission; final binding rechecks attach files.
- A person who loses attach files may still inspect or cancel their pending upload while they can see its channel. This lets the person release an unused upload without restoring the write permission.

Messages and threads:

- Sending or editing a top-level channel message requires send messages. A thread reply requires send messages in threads instead, on both send and edit. Direct conversations retain their participant rules.
- The first reply additionally requires create threads. A stored reply or a retained thread writer record establishes an existing thread, including after its last reply was deleted. Following an empty root does not establish a thread.
- Adding a reaction requires add reactions. Removing one's existing reaction remains allowed.
- Adding an ordinary role mention on send or edit requires mention roles. The everyone role and the everyone mention require mention everyone. An unchanged mention retained by an edit requires no new grant and sends no new mention notice.

Message reads:

- Channel and thread history require read message history. A single channel message also requires it unless the message was created strictly after the current subscribed event connection began. The server records this time; requests cannot supply it.
- Message event audiences retain the original message creation time, including system lines and deleted replies whose rows are erased. An edit or deletion time must not make older content qualify as a live message. Deletion records its event in the same transaction before erasing the message row.
- Message change events use this creation-time rule when replay is counted, when events are delivered and when a request receipt is selected. A retained old event without creation metadata requires history permission. Current visibility and the saved readers of private system lines remain required.
- The live exception uses the latest subscribed connection for the requesting session and device. A replaced, unsubscribed or stopping connection grants no exception. Reconnecting establishes a new boundary. Current view channel permission is always required.
- Search results and unread thread lists include only channels with read message history. Scoped search, pins, thread history and a thread copy require that permission explicitly. An export checks both export channel and read message history when requested, while being built and during download.
- Saved notices, their pagination cursors, read updates and notification events require history permission or a message created after the current live connection began. The SQL applies this bound before pagination and counting. A snapshot retains live notices and the settings of visible channels without exposing old notices or old unread threads.
- Recordings and transcripts require read message history, including their files, corrections and management routes. Starting and stopping a recording retain the separate in-call and record calls requirements.
- An attachment download uses the same creation-time bound as its message, including when permission is checked again during a running download. A reconnect can therefore end a history-free download of an older message.
- Sending and editing return the written message without requiring read message history. Internal checks of a reply destination and updates of its root do not turn an authorized write into a history read. Direct conversations retain their participant and departure rules.
- A change of a message that is older than the current connection, in a channel that the recipient sees without read message history, reaches the connection as `channel_history_withdrawn` with only the workspace and the channel, at most once per change and channel, in live delivery and in replay; replay counting and request receipts count it in the same way. The event of the creation of an older message and a mention send nothing, because the connection cannot hold that message. Reason: a client kept deleted or edited old content, and no event could tell the change without an old message identifier. Tests: `history_permissions::withdrawn`.

Message edits:

- Editing a message is denied when the message is permanent, when the caller is not its author, and in a direct message when the caller is not a participant. These are limits. No permission lets a person edit the message of another person.

Stored sets:

- A set of permissions is stored as one integer in which each permission has a fixed bit. A test fixes the bit and the name of every permission.
- The database refuses an integer with a bit above the highest known one. A new permission needs a migration that widens that check.
- Migration `0042_person_and_category_permissions` stores server, workspace, category and channel person overrides, category role overrides, and whether a channel is synced. Composite foreign keys keep roles and categories or channels in the correct workspace. Sets are disjoint and nonempty; removing the last bit removes the row. Workspace sets use bits 0–30.
- Existing everyone roles gain the channel-use permissions that previously followed visibility, including ordinary role mentions and voice status. Existing manage-channel grants and overrides also grant or deny setting the call user limit. Existing channel overrides stay detached; channels without overrides start synced with their initially empty category. The event epoch changes so a client reads the new permission lists.
- A synced channel reads its category role and person overrides. Its first actual override change copies all of those rules into the channel and detaches it in the same transaction. An identical request keeps it synced. Deleting a category first detaches its children, preserving their permissions, then removes the category, and sends `channel_rules_synced` with `synced` false for each child that was synced.

Rules of categories and channels:

- A rule has a subject, which is a workspace role (`{"kind": "role", "role_id": ...}`) or one member of the workspace (`{"kind": "person", "account_id": ...}`), an allow set and a deny set of workspace permissions. Two empty sets mean that the subject has no rule; storing them removes the row.
- `GET /api/v1/channels/{channel_id}/rules` returns `ChannelRules`: the channel, its category, `synced`, and the rules that the channel uses. `synced` is true when the channel has a category and takes its rules; the rules are then the rules of the category. It requires manage channel in the channel.
- `PUT` and `PATCH` on `/api/v1/channels/{channel_id}/rules/roles/{role_id}` and on `/api/v1/channels/{channel_id}/rules/people/{account_id}` set both sets (`SetChannelOverride`) or one permission to `allow`, `deny` or `inherit` (`ChangeChannelOverride`), preserving every other bit. They return the `PermissionRule`. They require manage channel in the channel. The channel row is locked and the change is made in the serializable transaction; retries preserve concurrent changes of other permissions.
- `GET /api/v1/workspaces/{workspace_id}/categories/{category_id}/rules` returns `CategoryRules`, and `PUT` and `PATCH` on `.../rules/roles/{role_id}` and `.../rules/people/{account_id}` change the rules of the category in the same way. They require manage channel in the category, which is evaluated with the rules of the category. A change of the rules of a category reaches every synced channel of it at once, because a synced channel reads the rules of its category.
- The role of a role rule must be a role of the workspace of the place. The person of a person rule must be a member of that workspace. Otherwise the answer is `not_found`, the same as for an absent account. For a person rule the caller must outrank the person in the workspace (ADR 0309) or be the person.
- `POST /api/v1/channels/{channel_id}/rules/sync` removes the own rules of a channel, which then takes the rules of its category again, and returns `ChannelRules`. A channel without a category gets `invalid_request`. A synced channel is returned as it is and nothing is written.
- Moving a synced channel into another category makes it take the rules of the destination category. The own rules that it had are removed. Moving a synced channel out of its category to no category copies the rules of the category into the channel first, so that the move does not change who can see and use it; the channel is then not synced. Moving a channel that is not synced keeps its own rules. A channel without a category that never had rules counts as synced, so it takes the rules of the category that it moves into.
- A hidden channel or category, and an absent one, get `not_found` on every rule route. A caller who sees the place but does not hold manage channel gets `forbidden`.
- A request that leaves a rule as it is, and an override of a structure file (ADR 0212) that leaves it as it is, writes nothing: no audit entry, no system line and no event, and a synced channel stays synced. Reason: the `PUT` route and the structure file wrote an audit entry and a line for a request that changed nothing.
- Each change has an audit entry: `channel_override_set` for a role rule of a channel, `channel_person_rule_set`, `category_role_rule_set`, `category_person_rule_set`, and `channel_rules_synced` when a channel detaches, is synced again or moves into another category while synced. The entry of a channel names the channel; the entry of a category names the workspace and has the category identifier.
- Each change sends the event `permission_rule_set` with the workspace, the place (`{"kind": "channel", ...}` or `{"kind": "category", ...}`), the subject and both sets. Its audience is the people who hold manage channel in that channel or category, decided at delivery. `channel_rules_synced` has the audience of the channel. A client updates the rules that it holds from `permission_rule_set`, also for a synced channel of the category that the event names, and reads the rules of a channel again after `channel_rules_synced`.
- The people whom a change concerns get `permissions_changed`: the holders of the role of a role rule, the person of a person rule, and every member of the workspace after a sync or a move that changes the rules of a channel. The rooms of the calls of the changed channels are marked, so that the next admission check reads the new rules (ADR 0509).
- Access tokens (ADR 0210) reach the reading routes with `read_structure` and the role-rule routes and the sync route with `manage_structure`. No access token reaches a person-rule route. The reading routes and the sync route leave the rules of people out of their answer for an access token. A sync by an access token, as a sync in a structure file, replaces the own person rules of the channel by those of its category; the plan of the file states it.
- The deletion event of a channel (`channel_deleted`) reaches the accounts that could see the channel at its deletion. Its audience stores the role rules and the person rules that applied to the channel, which are the rules of the category while the channel is synced, and evaluates them with the permission function when the event is delivered or replayed.
- An audit entry about a category (`category_created`, `category_updated`, `category_deleted`, `category_role_rule_set`, `category_person_rule_set`) is returned to a reader who can see the category now, as an entry about a channel is returned to a reader who can see the channel. The entries of a category that is deleted are returned only to administrators, as the entries of a deleted channel are (ADR 0303).
- A call state report needs the permissions of the channel of the call: reporting a screen share needs share screen, and also speak when the share sends sound; reporting a microphone that is on needs speak. A request without them answers `forbidden`.

Member access:

- `GET /api/v1/workspaces/{workspace_id}/members/{account_id}/access` returns, for each channel of the workspace that the caller can see, the permissions for which the permission function allows a request of the member there (`ChannelPermissions`). A channel that the member cannot see is listed with no permission. A channel that the caller cannot see is not listed, so the answer does not tell that it exists. It requires manage roles in the workspace; an account that is not a member gets `not_found`.
- `PATCH /api/v1/workspaces/{workspace_id}/channels/positions` takes 1 to 200 moves (the constant `MOST_CHANNEL_MOVES` of the protocol, which the window reads to offer no larger move, ADR 0761), each naming a channel of the workspace once, the category and the position where the client read it, and its new category and its new position or place (below). One serializable transaction first checks every channel: it exists and the caller holds view channel in it, both answered with `not_found`, so that the answer does not tell whether a channel that the caller does not see exists or in which workspace it is; it belongs to the workspace; the caller holds manage channel in it; it is not archived; and its category and the position that the caller reads equal those of the move; then it passes every moved channel through the function of the route of one channel, which moves a channel that changes its group to the end of the new group and records its `channel_updated` event, sets the order of each destination group once from all the moves of the request into it, which a client of API v1 computed together, and records the audit entry of each moved channel with its position after the request. A failed check ends the request, `conflict` for a channel that is no longer where the move read it, and nothing changes. A move whose new category and position equal those where the client read it is a check only: the caller needs view channel in it and not manage channel, and it is not written, so it has no audit entry and no event, also in a request of checks only. A client sends the channels next to the destination that it read as such checks: the place of a moved channel is computed from its neighbours, and a move that names only the channels that change would be applied after another person moved a neighbour, to a place that the client did not choose. A change of the order that touches several channels is therefore applied whole or not at all, and only to the order that the client computed it from. Tests: `structure::channels_are_moved_together_or_not_at_all`, `structure::a_move_is_refused_when_a_neighbour_that_it_checks_has_moved`.

- The read of one channel with the standing of the caller (`Access::channel`) runs the same statements for a channel that does not exist as for one that the caller may not see: the channel, the roles and the membership of its workspace (of a workspace that does not exist, for an absent channel), and the overrides of the channel; both answer `not_found`. Reason: an absent channel answered after one statement and a hidden one after four, and repeated requests could tell them apart by the time of the answer. Test: `messages_access::an_absent_and_a_hidden_channel_run_the_same_statements` compares the statements of the two requests in the statistics of the database. Only the absence of the workspace is kept until the overrides are read; a failure of the database in the read of the workspace ends the request at once, because a later statement on the failed transaction fails as an aborted transaction, which is not retried, and would replace the failure (a serialization failure or the lock time limit) with an internal error. Test: `time_limits::a_channel_whose_workspace_read_waits_too_long_gets_the_timeout_error` reads and sends a message while `workspace_roles` is locked and expects the time-limit error.

## Consequences

- One test suite of table tests and property tests covers the rule for every route.
- A person with "manage roles" cannot use a role to enter a channel: assigning a role whose override allows "view channel" in a channel that the person cannot see is denied, and so is removing a role whose override denies "view channel" there, from the own account or from another member.
- The roles and overrides that a decision uses are read in the transaction of the change (ADR 0009), so a permission that was revoked before the change commits is not used.
- A person with "manage channel" who denies a permission to a role that the person holds loses that permission in the channel and cannot remove the deny again. An administrator can.
- Role changes and server moderation also obey the order of ADR 0309. A person with "manage roles" changes only roles below their highest role, with the permission-grant ceiling above.
- A person with "create workspace" and no administrator permission gets an empty workspace that an administrator has to give roles to.
- A person with manage channel cannot use a person rule, a role rule, a sync or a move to give anyone, the own account included, a permission that the person does not hold, or a channel that the person cannot see.
- A category is listed only when a person sees a channel in it that is neither archived nor deleted, or can manage the category. The same rule decides the snapshot, the channel list, the category routes and the audience of category events, so an archived channel makes no category visible. Its management permission is evaluated with its role and person overrides. Renaming or deleting a hidden category, and creating or moving a channel into one, answers as for a missing category. A person who can manage one category can create channels there without workspace-wide management permission.
- The position of a category or a channel that a person reads (`access::order`): a person who sees every entry of the group, every category of the workspace or every channel of a category or of the root that is not deleted, archived ones included, reads the stored positions, which hide nothing from that person. Any other person reads the place of each entry that the person sees among those entries, counted from 0. The channel list, the snapshot, the answers of the category and channel routes, the events `category_created`, `category_updated`, `channel_created`, `channel_updated` and `channel_restored` at their delivery or replay, and the check of `from` of the positions route use this rule. An archived channel is in no list and has the position 0 in the archive. The structure document of ADR 0211, which only an administrator reads, gives the stored positions, of archived channels too.
- A request names a new position in one of two ways. `position` (`CreateCategory`, `UpdateCategory`, `CreateChannel`, `UpdateChannel`, `to` of a move, and the changes of a structure file) keeps the meaning of API v1: a position in the caller's view as the caller reads positions; the entries that the caller sees are ordered by position and identifier with the new values of the request. For a caller who sees every entry, the position is stored as given and the group is not numbered again, exactly as API v1 stored it, so several requests computed from one read, as the window of release 0.1.0 sends for Up and Down of a category and as the setup tool sends for a file, end in the order that the client computed. A group is never numbered again in the middle of a request or of a structure apply (below), so the later positions of the same request or file refer to the numbers that the client read. For a caller who sees fewer entries the same order of the caller's view is computed from the caller's places, the hidden entries are placed as below, and the group is numbered again from 0; such a caller reads places, which change after each request, so a client of release 0.1.0 of such a caller that needs several requests for one move of a category can end at another place than it drew (the limit of ADR 0206). `place` (`UpdateCategory`, `to` of a move; the window of this release) is the place among the entries that the caller sees after the change, counted from 0; a place past the last puts the entry after the last.
- An entry that the caller does not see stays before the entry that the caller sees after it, or, when the request moves that one, before the next one that the request does not move; without one it stays at the end. A request whose result keeps the order that the caller sees changes nothing and records nothing, so that it does not tell the caller whether a hidden entry lies next to the entry, also for a creation at the end of the caller's view. A channel that changes its group without a position goes after the last channel of the new group, and the channels of a deleted category go after the channels of the root, in their order: one statement numbers the root and those channels from 0, adding nothing to a stored position, so a position near the largest one that the API accepts does not make the deletion fail.
- The stored positions of a group never keep the place of an entry that left it: the deletion of a channel, the deletion of an archived channel at the end of its archive period and the move of a channel to another group number the remaining channels of the group again from 0 in their stored order; the deletion of a category numbers the categories again. A group is numbered once, at the end of the request or of the structure apply (`routes::channels::Departures`), for every group that an entry left during it, and never between two changes of the same request or file. Reason: a person who sees every remaining entry reads the stored positions, and a gap could show where an entry hidden from that person was; a renumbering between two changes of one request or file would change the numbers that its later positions refer to. Numbering every such group, and not only the groups whose leaving entry a rule may have hidden, needs no reading of the rules during the transaction, which a role or rule change earlier in the same structure apply would already have changed. A person whose view of a group changes between every entry and fewer reads other numbers when the stored positions of the listed entries are not 0 to n-1 (an archived channel before a listed one, or a position stored as given): a creation of an entry that some person may not see (a category, which is listed only to the people who manage channels while it is empty, or a channel that a rule may hide by the conservative condition of `events::log::may_be_hidden`: the channel is in a category, has rules, the workspace has person rules, or its everyone role lacks "view channel"), and a departure of such a channel that leaves such numbers, record a refresh of the workspace in that case; a departure that changes a stored position records one always. The change of the numbers then tells a person who saw every entry that an entry hidden from that person was added to or left the group; it tells neither where nor which. Tests: `order_compat::an_entry_that_leaves_a_group_leaves_no_gap`, `order_compat::a_new_entry_hidden_from_a_person_refreshes_the_workspace`, `order_compat::a_structure_apply_numbers_a_group_that_a_channel_left_after_its_last_change`, `order_compat::the_channels_of_a_deleted_category_go_after_the_root_in_their_order`.
- The setup tool of this release compares the order of the entries of each group, not their numbers (`junto-setup` `plan`): when the entries of a group that the file gives a position are in the order of the file on the server and none of them enters the group, it sends no position, also when the numbers differ, for example by the gap of a deleted entry that the server numbered away; otherwise it sends the place of each such entry among them, counted from 0, for each entry whose stored position differs, and for a channel that enters the group. So a file applied again plans no change after the server numbered a group, and does not write back a gap. The setup tool of release 0.1.0 compares the numbers and sends the numbers of the file, which the server stores as given for an administrator: a file applied again with that tool can write back the gap of a deleted entry. A new workspace keeps its AFK channel at position 0, and the default workspace of a new server at 2, after its two first channels. Tests: `setup_tool::a_file_applied_again_does_not_write_back_the_gap_of_a_deleted_channel`, the unit tests `numbers_that_keep_the_order_change_nothing` and `another_order_sends_the_places_of_the_entries_that_move` of `junto-setup`.
- The cost of the reads: a position that a person reads needs the visibility of every channel of the workspace for that person. The route of one channel reads it once for its answer and, when the request names a position, once more for the order of the group; the positions route once for the checks at its start, once per destination group and once for the audit entries, not once per channel; the delivery of channel events reads it once per workspace and recipient.
- Every change of the stored order of a group records one `permissions_changed` for the workspace, so every person reads the structure again: the places of other entries changed for a person who does not see every entry. A channel of the root that leaves the lists by an archive or a deletion, or enters them by a restore, records one as well when an open channel follows it in the root; a channel of a category records one for the category already.
- The audit entries `category_updated` and `channel_updated` keep the position in the view of the actor. A reader of the audit log holds "view audit entries" and can learn from such a position that the actor sees more categories or channels than the reader. The entry records the request of the actor; a position computed for each reader would state another request.
- Reason for the places: the stored positions were sent to every person, so a gap in the positions that a person read could tell how many categories or channels were hidden from that person. Reason for the two meanings of a request: the meaning of `position` is the one that clients of release 0.1.0 compute, and ADR 0206 needs a new field for a changed meaning. Tests: `category_order::*`, `order_compat::*` (the Up and Down and the drag of the window of release 0.1.0, copied from it, a request that keeps the caller's view, the removal of a root channel), `setup_tool::a_file_orders_the_categories_and_the_root_channels_exactly`, and the unit tests of `access::order`.
- Category creation and rename events use the current category visibility at delivery and replay. A category deletion sends a workspace permission refresh without its identifier, so a person who never saw it learns no hidden name or identifier.
- A channel with a category that leaves the lists by a deletion or an archive can be the last channel of the category that a person sees. The change sends a workspace permission refresh, as a move of a channel to another category does, so that the client reads the categories again and no longer holds a category that the server does not list. A channel that was already archived sends none when it is deleted. One change records the refresh of one audience once, also when it moves, deletes or archives many channels: before, one request of 200 moves sent 200 refreshes to every person of the workspace. The refreshes that a transaction recorded are kept in a setting of the transaction, which ends with it and is empty again when a serialization failure runs it again (ADR 0009). The rooms of the calls are marked for the admission check at every change of permissions of the transaction, also when its refresh was recorded before by a new order of categories, which marks no room (ADR 0509). Tests: `category_refresh::a_move_after_a_new_order_of_categories_in_one_request_marks_the_room_of_the_call`, `category_refresh::one_request_of_several_moves_between_categories_sends_one_refresh`, `category_refresh::deleting_the_last_child_refreshes_people_who_lose_visibility`, `archiving_the_last_child_refreshes_people_who_lose_visibility`, `a_category_whose_last_channel_is_archived_sends_its_new_name_only_to_managers`.
- The number of a permission bit cannot be reused for another permission.

Mention events retain the message creation time, including mentions added by editing. Delivery and replay require read message history unless that creation time is after the verified current live connection start; adding a mention does not make an older message readable. Legacy mention audiences without a creation time require history.
