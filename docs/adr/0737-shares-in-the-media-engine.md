# 0737. Shares in the media engine: the tracks of a share, the watched share and its sound

## Status

Accepted

Open: the call library (`livekit` 0.9.4) sends a sound track with one channel. It has no setting for stereo when it uses two peer connections, and its mode with one peer connection did not give two channels either: the request that adds a track does not state stereo. The sound of a share therefore arrives as the mean of its two channels. The pipeline of the client is stereo up to the call library.

Resolved by ADR 0773: shares use one encoding at full resolution. The former two-layer policy could leave a viewer receiving only the half-size layer after a bandwidth reduction; there is no lower-resolution layer to select under the current policy.

Open: after a viewer chooses another share, the full size can arrive late. In seven runs of the live test in the Linux container, with the two layers that a share had then, the full size at the full frame rate began in the first second six times. Once the viewer got 16 pictures of the full size in the first second, none for six seconds, and the full rate from the eighth second on. The cause is not established. The media server decided which layer it sent to a viewer; since ADR 0773 a share has one encoding of the full size, and the measurement was not repeated on Linux with it before the two changes were joined.

## Context

ADR 0502 decides that several people can share at once, that each viewer picks the share to watch and hears the sound of that share, that a viewer can turn the sound of a share off or change its volume and keep the picture, and that shared sound is its own track, at a higher bitrate and without noise suppression and echo cancellation. ADR 0510 decides that the server shows a share from the report of the sharing device while the media server lists its picture track, and that a microphone block closes the sound of a share. ADR 0602 lets the layout page of the recorder ask for the quality layer of the size at which a picture is drawn. The engine carries one media connection (ADR 0733), and the calls of a client join it with the rules of a server (ADR 0734).

## Decision

The two-layer publication policy below is superseded by ADR 0773. Capture ownership, audio and ordered viewer requests remain as described here.

The shares of this device:

- A device runs up to three shares at once (ADR 0502), each in a slot from 0 to 2; on a server from before several shares, which states no number in its answer, it runs one (ADR 0510). The engine takes the number from each join and states it (`EngineState::share_limit`); the menu of the own shares disables "Share another" at that number. A start takes the lowest free slot and leaves the shares that run; a start while as many run starts nothing and states `TooMany` as the end of a share, which the window tells. Test: `engine::share::tests::a_start_takes_the_lowest_free_slot_and_no_slot_beyond_the_limit`. A stop names a slot, or ends every share without one. A share that ends by itself, or whose capture does not start, ends only its slot. The handle of the engine counts the stops of each slot, so that the frames of one share are dropped at its stop while the other shares go on (ADR 0794). Each share has its own capture, its own picture track and its own sound track; what is said below of a share holds for each of them.

- The picture is one video track with the source "screen share", sent in two layers: the full size at the frame rate that the sharer chose, and half the size at no more than 15 pictures per second. The bitrate is 3 Mbit/s for 1920 by 1080 at 30 pictures per second, in proportion to the pixels and, for half of it, to the frame rate, between 150 kbit/s and 8 Mbit/s. The defaults of the call library are not used: they send a share of less than 1920 pixels at 15 or at 5 pictures per second. When the processor of the sharer is busy, the call library lowers the frame rate and keeps the size of the picture: the client states the degradation preference "maintain resolution" itself and does not rely on the default of the call library for a screen share. The sound is one audio track with the source "screen share audio": 48 kHz, two channels, 128 kbit/s, no processing, no silence suppression and no redundant copy.
- The codec of the picture of the own share and of the own camera depends on the system and on the size and frame rate of the picture (`engine::sent::encoder`). On macOS it is H.264, encoded by the encoder of the system hardware (VideoToolbox, the encoder backend `VideoToolbox` of the vendored WebRTC wrapper, which wraps it in its simulcast adapter for the layers of the camera), when the picture is within level 3.1 of H.264: 3,600 blocks of 16 × 16 pixels, 108,000 blocks per second and 169 blocks on a side, such as 1280 × 720 at 30 pictures per second. A larger picture is sent in VP8 at its full size (ADR 0773). The camera, at most 1280 × 720 at 30 pictures per second, is within the level; a share of 1920 × 1080 or at 60 pictures per second is not.
- The limit is the level that the media server answers, not the level of the hardware (5.2). The pinned LiveKit server 1.13.9 offers H.264 only in its built-in format lines: `profile-level-id` 42001f, 42e01f and 4d001f (level 3.1) and 640032 (High profile, level 5.0). Its configuration (`room.enabled_codecs`) can enable or disable these lines and cannot set another level, and it matches an offered H.264 format by its profile bytes, which for the encoder of VideoToolbox (Constrained Baseline 42e0, Constrained High 640c) leaves only level 3.1. WebRTC sends at the level of the answer, and the encoder of VideoToolbox sets its expected frame rate from that level: 13 for 1920 × 1080. So a picture above level 3.1 would be encoded with rate settings for another frame rate than its own, and the stream would not keep the level that it states to its receivers. Within the level both hold. `deploy/livekit-common.yaml` therefore sets no codec (ADR 0904). A media server that answers a higher level, through a later version or its own format lines, lets the limit rise to that level.
- Windows and Linux send VP8, encoded in software, as before; their WebRTC library offers no hardware encoder that this client uses. Every receiver decodes both codecs: macOS with VideoToolbox, Windows and Linux with the H.264 decoder of FFmpeg that their WebRTC library links (`rtc_use_h264` with `ffmpeg_branding = "Chrome"` in the `args.gn` of the archives of `webrtc-89d790b`), and the recorder with its browser (the deployment recording test publishes a camera and a share in H.264). The call library offers only the formats of the chosen codec for a track (`set_codec_preferences` in `rtc_session.rs`), so the codec is chosen before the publication. An encoder of VideoToolbox that does not start or fails is replaced by the software H.264 encoder of WebRTC (OpenH264) in the same track (ADR 0733, software fallback of a hardware encoder). The effect on the processor and the memory is to be measured with the `modes` target on macOS (ADR 0914) and recorded in `docs/client-performance.md`.
- Tests: `engine::sent::tests::a_picture_beyond_the_negotiated_level_of_h264_is_encoded_in_vp8_at_its_size` (every system) checks the choice at the limits; `engine::sent::tests::every_system_decodes_the_codecs_that_the_systems_encode` (every system, in each CI job) checks that the receiver capabilities hold H.264 and VP8 and the sender capabilities the codec of the system for a picture within level 3.1 (H.264 on macOS); `encoder_fallback::a_hardware_encoder_that_does_not_start_falls_back_to_the_software_encoder_of_its_codec` (every system) checks the fallback; the media live test `share::on_macos_the_own_share_and_camera_are_encoded_by_the_hardware_of_the_system` (macOS) checks that each encoding that sends pictures of a share of 1280 × 720 at 30 pictures per second and of the camera names VideoToolbox and a power-efficient encoder, that the codec in use is H.264, and that the other person shows both pictures; the media live test `share::a_share_larger_than_the_levels_of_h264_arrives_at_its_size_in_vp8` (every system) checks a share of 5120 × 2880.
- The name of the picture track states what is shared, at which frame rate and in which slot: `screen/30` or `window/30` for slot 0, which a client of one share reads, and `screen/30/1` or `window/30/2` for slots 1 and 2. The server reads the slot from it (ADR 0510). The sound track of slot 0 is named `share-sound`, of slot 1 `share-sound/1`. A picture track with another name states nothing and is the share of slot 0; a sound track with another name is the sound of slot 0. The title of a shared window is sent to nobody.
- Each share has an identifier that the engine makes at its start (a UUID). A share runs when its capture has started and its picture track is published. The calls then report it to the server with its slot, its identifier, its kind and its sound choice, and report the end of its slot when it stops (ADR 0510). A share that the server holds in a slot is the same share only with the same identifier: a share that ends and a new one with the same sound and kind in its slot, while a report of another slot is on its way, is reported as the new share, and the server gives it its own start. Test: `calls::tests::a_new_share_in_the_slot_of_an_ended_one_is_reported_after_a_delayed_report`. One request is sent at a time, about one slot: first a share that runs and that the server does not hold as it runs, then the end of a slot that the server holds and that does not run, each in the order of the slots. A device that replaces one share by another therefore reports the new share before the end of the old one, and a presenter of meeting mode holds a share at the server all the time (ADR 0512). Two reports are at least 1 second apart, so that they and the reports of the own state (ADR 0734) stay within the 30 reports per 10 seconds of the server. After a request that failed, the server may hold the share of its slot or not: that slot is told again, its share or its end, after 3 seconds. A new seat holds no share at the server. Test: `calls::tests::each_share_is_reported_by_its_slot_one_request_at_a_time`.
- The start of the capture belongs to the share. A share that the person stops while the system still prepares its capture ends that work, and what the system had started is stopped.
- When the call library connects anew, it withdraws each track of the device and publishes it again under another identifier. A track of the share that was withdrawn without a request of the share is waited for, for 10 seconds; the capture goes on. Only the track with the identifier that the share sent returns to it. A picture track that does not return ends the share. A sound track that does not return is published anew when the person may send it. A track that returns for a share that was stopped or replaced is withdrawn. The microphone track is handled in the same way (ADR 0733).
- While the capture gives no picture, the last picture is sent again once a second. A screen that does not change sends nothing otherwise, and a person who starts to watch would get no picture.
- The sound track is published while the share runs with a sound and the permission of the participant allows the source. A microphone block of the server withdraws it; the picture stays, and the sharer is told that the sound is closed. Without the block it is published again.
- A share ends when the person stops it, when the capture ends by itself, when the person leaves the call, and when the media connection is lost for good; the last two end every share. A share does not start again by itself.
- A capture that fails to start, a share that ended by itself, and a start beyond three shares are told with their reason until the person starts a share, stops one, or puts the statement away. Putting the statement away leaves the shares that run.
- When the call library publishes a track of this device again, the share whose picture or sound waits for the previous identifier, or holds it, takes the new publication; every other share keeps its tracks.

The shares of the other people:

- While a device has two or more shares or share tracks on the way to the media server, a participant of the room receives its share tracks other than those of the share of the lowest slot that runs only when its attribute `junto-shares` states two or more or when it is of the kind egress, a recorder (ADR 0510). A share counts from the start of the publication of its picture until it ends. A share track that the call library publishes and that no share holds also counts: a sound whose publication has not reached the engine, a track published again under a new identifier that the engine has not taken yet, a track of an ended share that is not withdrawn yet. A track of an ended share whose publication has not answered counts as well. The engine then sets the track permissions of the media server as lists: each such participant receives every track; every other participant receives only the tracks that its list names, the tracks of the device that are not share tracks and the published picture and sound of the share of the lowest slot that runs. The media server withholds a track that is published later from that participant until the engine adds it to the list, so a share track that the engine has not placed never reaches it. With fewer, every participant receives every track.
- The picture and the sound of a share are published only after the call library has taken permissions that count the share. The engine numbers each list that it sends; one task per room connection gives the lists to the call library in order, the latest of those that wait first, and tells the engine the number of each list that the call library took. The call library writes the permissions to the signal connection before the request that publishes a track after them, and keeps a request that waits for the connection in the same queue. A share whose permissions are not taken within 10 seconds, the limit of a publication, or whose permissions the call library refused, ends with an error that the sharer sees; it is not published without them.
- The engine works the list out again when a participant comes, goes or changes its attributes, when a track of the device is published, withdrawn or published again, and when a share changes; it sends it only when it differs from the list sent last, and again after the session of the call library has resumed or connected anew. A participant who comes in while the lists are in force receives no track of the device until the list of its arrival is sent; a participant whose attributes state two or more shares is given every track by the list of its attributes.
- A resume keeps the session at the media server with its permissions, and the call library sends them again after it. After a new connection of the session the media server holds a new session without the earlier permissions (read from the call library, not measured: that it then lets every participant receive every track), and the call library publishes every track again; the vendored call library sends the permissions that it holds before the first of those tracks (ADR 0733). They name the earlier identifiers, so a participant with a list receives no track of the device until the engine has taken the new identifiers and sent the new list. A new connection of the engine with a new token ends every share (above).
- Tests: `engine::permissions::tests`: a share whose picture is being published counts toward two shares, and a client of one share is never given a share track that the engine has not placed, a share track of a slot above the lowest, or a track of an ended share. The media live test `share::a_further_share_is_published_only_after_the_permissions_that_withhold_it` holds the permissions of the sharer back (`Calls::hold_permissions`): the media server holds no track of the further share while they are held, a hold longer than the limit ends that share with an error while the first share goes on, and after a shorter hold the further share is published and a connection of the call library that states nothing is never given one of its tracks. The media live test `share::a_client_of_a_release_before_several_shares_never_receives_a_further_share` runs the test participant of a release from before several shares as that client (`JUNTO_TEST_LEGACY_PARTICIPANT` names its program; without it the test states that it is skipped): it receives the share of the lowest slot, and is never given a track of the further share while that share starts with the permissions held back for 3 seconds, while both shares run, and while the call library of the sharer connects anew and publishes every track again. `share::a_client_of_one_share_receives_one_of_several_shares_and_they_end_with_their_sharer` checks with the packets that arrive on each track that such a client receives the share of the lowest slot with its sound while two shares run (the call library keeps a track after the media server withdrew the permission to receive it), the share that is left when one ends, the share of the lowest slot after the call library of the sharer has connected anew, and no share after the sharer has left; the viewer of several shares sees both and hears the one that it watches.
- A share exists for a viewer when the picture track of a person with an account is received. It is identified by its person and its slot, so two shares of one person are two shares. The shares are kept in the order in which they were received.
- The viewer watches one share: the one that the viewer chose while it runs; otherwise the share of another person that was received first; otherwise the own share of the lowest slot. A share that starts does not replace the share of another person that the viewer watches. It replaces the preview of the own share, unless the viewer chose the preview. When the chosen share ends, the rule without a choice applies; another share of the same person is not chosen for that reason. Test: `engine::share::tests::the_watched_share_is_the_choice_while_it_runs_then_the_first_other_then_the_own`.
- The picture of the watched share is made into planes of brightness and colour at the size at which the interface draws it (the form of a camera picture, ADR 0774), at most 30 times a second; a picture that arrives earlier waits, so the last picture of a share is always drawn. A small picture of every share is made once a second. The interface tells the engine the size of its drawing in pixels.
- The engine asks the media server for each picture in the layer of its drawn size: the watched share at the size of the interface, every other share at the size of its small picture. It asks when the size for a share changes and not otherwise; a change of the share volume asks for nothing. The requests reach the media server in the order in which the engine made them. The call library sends each request from a task that it starts for that request, and it gives no handle to wait for: on a runtime with several threads two requests that are made at the same moment arrive in any order, and the media server keeps the size that arrived last. The engine therefore makes these requests on one thread of the process, whose runtime runs its tasks in the order in which they were started. A request that panics does not end that thread. Without the thread, which the system can refuse, a request is made from the caller and the order is not kept; the engine logs that.
- After a viewer chooses another share, the live test waits until the media connection of the viewer receives the picture of the full size at 25 or more pictures a second, as the statistics of its receiver state, and prints the time from the change. From then on, the three following seconds must give the viewer at least 66 pictures of the full size. A layer of half the size, which a peer with the two-layer policy publishes, gives at most 45 pictures in three seconds. The time until the media connection receives the full rate depends on the estimate of the bandwidth of the sharer and on the media server; the test has no bound of its own for it, so that a slow host does not decide the result. A full rate that never arrives ends the test at its limit. One cause ends it earlier, with its statement: when, 20 seconds after the change, the sender states that its processor holds the full-size picture below 25 pictures a second (quality limitation `Cpu`) and has held it for more than half of the time of the encoding, the full rate cannot arrive. A run of the Linux container with two build processors for the three call engines of the test is such a run: there the sender encodes 2 pictures a second, on main as on this design, and the test needs a run with more processors.
- The first share with sound: the live test waits until the output of the viewer plays the tone of the share, and from then on requires that half a second of the output holds the tone at its level. When the sound track reaches the viewer after the picture track depends on the media server.
- The sound of a share is played only while its share is watched, with the share volume of the viewer (0 to 200 %, where 0 keeps the picture without the sound). The sound track of every other share, also of another share of the same person, is received and has no volume in the mix. The engine does not end and start subscriptions with the choice of the viewer: the call library sends each such change by itself, in no fixed order, and a sound track is a small part of what a share costs. The volume of a sound is set in the mix at once, also for what the mix already holds.
- The sharer gets the pictures of the own share as a preview, at most 15 times a second, and never its sound.
- The level of a person and the speaking mark are measured from the microphone track only.

Sound of a share before the call library:

- The capture gives blocks of any size at its own rate. They become frames of 10 ms at 48 kHz with two channels: one channel is sent on both, and of more than two the first two. Up to half a second waits when the media connection takes the frames late; a further frame is dropped.

## Consequences

- A viewer of two shares receives two picture tracks, one of them in its small layer, and both sound tracks, of which one is played. This holds for two shares of one person as for the shares of two people.
- The media live test `share::one_person_shares_two_things_and_the_viewer_hears_only_the_one_watched` runs a screen with all system sound and a window with the sound of one application of one person: the viewer sees both shares with their kinds and slots, hears only the sound of the watched one, switches to the other and back, sees a small picture of each, and the server lists both; a third share takes slot 2, a fourth is refused while three run; the end of the watched share makes the viewer watch the share of that person that was received first of those left, and a stop without a slot ends every share. The synthetic capture gives the window other colours (`SyntheticCapture::set_source_colours`), so that the picture of each share is told apart.
- A change of the watched share draws the last received picture of the new share at once.
- A person whose microphone is blocked shares pictures without sound, whatever client is used (ADR 0510).
- The sound of a share is sent while the person has muted the own microphone: the two are separate tracks.
- A share that ended because the media connection was lost is started again by the person after the call has returned.
- The recorder receives the share tracks as any participant does (ADR 0602).
- The preview of a shared screen that shows the client draws the client inside itself.
- The order of the requests for a size depends on how the call library sends a request (`livekit` 0.9.4: one task for each request, and every wait on the way to the connection serves the tasks in the order of their arrival). It is checked again with each new version of the call library; the test of the last size of a drawing fails when the order is lost.
