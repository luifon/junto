# 0742. Notices, notification settings and system notifications in the window

## Status

Accepted

## Context

ADR 0404 defines unread signs, the notification level, the mute and "do not disturb". ADR 0409 defines their routes: one notice per recipient and message without text and without a place, a level for the account on a server, for a workspace and for a channel, a mute and two mention switches for a channel, and "do not disturb" with a daily quiet time as data of the account on one server. ADR 0722 gives the window the notices of the state, the messages that they name, the notification settings and the answer whether the account is in its quiet time. gpui shows a notification of the operating system, takes one away by its tag, and tells a press on one; on macOS that works only from an application bundle, and the system asks the person for the permission at the first notification (ADR 0701).

The server marks every notice up to a time as read with one request, and states with the snapshot whether a workspace has a level of its administrators (ADR 0412, ADR 0792). It has no setting for a thread. The server tells no notice while the account is in a quiet time, so a client learns of the notices of that time when the quiet time has ended.

## Decision

List of notices:

- The channel column has an entry "Notifications" above the channels, with the count of the notices that the person has not read. It opens the notices of the shown server in the side panel. The panel shows one list: opening the notices closes a thread, the pinned messages and a transcript, and takes a search out of the panel.
- The list orders its notices as a message list orders its messages (ADR 0740): the oldest at the top and the newest at the bottom. A list shorter than its area starts at the top of the area, under its header; a longer one opens at its end, and while it stands at its end a new notice is drawn at the bottom in view (a gpui list aligned to its top that follows its end); a list that the person moved away from its end keeps the rows in view, and a new notice is added below them. Older notices, read with "Show older" at the top of the list, are added above the listed rows without moving the rows in view. The same holds for the list in the panel of a call and in the call window. Reason: the list showed the newest notice at the top, the opposite of every message list of the window. Test: `notices::the_notices_are_ordered_as_messages_with_the_newest_at_the_bottom`.
- A row shows the place of the message, its day, its author, its time, what the notice is (a mention, a direct message, a reply in a thread, a new message) and the start of its text. A notice whose message the server no longer has is not listed, and neither is one whose place the state does not hold. A notice whose message could not be read is listed without its text and leads nowhere.
- A channel that is in no channel list can be archived: the state knows an archived channel from the archived list of its workspace (ADR 0762). While the list is open, the window holds the archived list of each workspace that a notice names with such a channel, and a notice of an archived channel is listed with its place and leads to it. The list stops at the first notice whose archived list has not answered and is marked as reading, and "Show older" is not drawn meanwhile: the older notices are added above the listed rows when the list answers, so no row in view and no control moves (ADR 0730). A notice of a workspace that the state does not hold waits for nothing and is not listed. When the read of an archived list fails, its notices are not listed, and the area of the notices above the list states it with "Try again", which reads the list again.
- A press on a row shows the message in its place and marks the notice as read. The channel or the conversation is selected, the thread of a reply is opened over the list, and the messages around the message are read when the list of the place does not hold it. The list goes to the message and marks it, and does not follow new messages until the person brings it to its end (ADR 0740). The jump belongs to the place whose list shows the message: the thread for a reply, else the channel or the conversation. In that list, the rows shown while it holds neither the message nor the messages around its position, such as the end of the latest page before the read around the message arrives, do not move the read position, read no page and tell the client core no position, until the person goes to the end of the place; the other lists, such as the channel under the thread of a reply, are read as usual. Reason: the list showed the end of the latest page in the active window before the jump, and the whole place was marked read; a first correction waited for the list to make the jump, so a list that never holds the message, such as the channel under a reply, was never read while the jump held. Tests: `notices::after_a_jump_to_a_notice_the_wheel_to_the_end_reads_the_channel`, `notices::after_a_notice_of_a_reply_the_channel_under_the_thread_is_read`. The mark stays until the person selects another place. A second press on the same row goes to the message again.
- "Mark all as read" sends one request that marks every notice up to the newest unread notice that the state holds; a notice that arrives later stays unread. The panel shows the marking until the state holds the event of the change (ADR 0763), and a press meanwhile sends nothing. A refusal is stated in the panel.
- The panel offers "Mark all as read" while the state holds an unread notice whose message is not gone, also while the messages of the notices are read. After a reload of the state the messages that the notices name are kept for display and read again (ADR 0721), so the rows stay.
- The read mark of the notice of a row is sent by itself. A mark that is asked for while marks of the server are on their way follows them in a request of its own; a mark that waits or is on its way is not sent a second time. An answer "not found" is no failure: the notice is gone. A read mark of a notice does not move a read position.
- "Show older", above the oldest listed notice, reads the page of the notices older than the listed ones. It continues from the last notice that the state holds, because the server takes as the cursor of a page only a notice that the account reads now. When an event takes every held notice, such as the deletion of the workspace or of the channel of all of them, and the server holds more, no notice is left to continue from: the state reads the notices again from their first page, and the panel shows the list as reading and offers no "Show older" until it answers. An answer of the first page that was read before the deletion of a channel or a workspace can hold their notices, and the state does not place their messages any more: the first page is read again after that answer. The same holds for a page of older notices read before such a deletion: its last notice would be the cursor of the next page. When the server refuses the cursor of a further page as a wrong request, the notices are read again from their first page, so that no cursor that the server refuses stays. Tests: `discovery_tests::notices_that_an_event_takes_whole_are_read_again_when_the_server_holds_more`, `stream::tests::discovery::a_refused_cursor_of_older_notices_reads_the_first_page_again`. While the held notices give no listed row and the server holds more, as when their messages are gone or their archived list failed, the panel offers "Show older" in place of the sentence that there is no notice. Test: `notices::the_notices_of_other_places_are_listed_when_a_deleted_workspace_took_every_held_notice` fills the first page with the notices of a second workspace, deletes it, and finds the older notice of the first workspace listed.
- In the view of the call of this device the side panel is the panel of the call. The entry of the notices opens the list there, in place of the pane of the tab that the panel shows, and a press on a tab of the panel shows that pane again. A narrow window draws the panel of the call only while it is open, and the entry of the notices opens it at the list. The call window has no channel column: its header has a button with the bell beside the buttons of the tabs, which opens the panel at the list and carries the count of the notices that the person has not read. The call window lists the notices of the server of its call, whatever the main window shows, and holds the archived lists that its notices need (ADR 0762); a press on a row shows the message in the main window, brings that window to the front and marks the notice as read. The button with the bell is drawn also while the panel of a direct-message call waits for its conversation. Notices that are open beside a place close when the main area changes to the view of the call of this device because another place is shown or the call began, so the panel of the call shows its chat; in the call the entry opens them again. A direct-message call that returns from the call window to the conversation that the main area shows keeps them open. Reason: before this, the list could not be opened while the person was in the view of a call, and the call window showed neither the list nor the count. Tests: `call_notices::the_notices_open_in_the_panel_of_the_call`, `call_notices::the_call_window_counts_and_lists_the_notices` (a notice of an archived channel and a newer one in the call window, and a press on a row). The second failed with the list of the call window stopped at the notice of the archived channel and shown as reading: the archived list was held only for the list of the main window.
- The count of the entry and of the button is the count of the notices that the state holds and that the person has not read. The state reads the first page of the notices again after each event that changes a notice (ADR 0722), so the count follows the events of the server; a notice leaves the count when it is read under the rules of this record. A button draws a count at its corner (`Button::count`), and the count of another place, such as the join requests, uses the same element.

Notification settings:

- One dialog shows the settings of one scope: the server (the account on that server), a workspace, or a channel. The menu of the server opens the first two, and the menu of a channel the third.
- The level is one of "all messages", "mentions only" and "nothing". A workspace and a channel also have "default", which takes the level of the scope above (ADR 0409). The dialog states which level "default" gives now. The state holds the level of the administrators of each workspace, or that the workspace sets none (ADR 0412, ADR 0792); with none, the level of the account holds.
- The dialog of a channel also has the mute with its four lengths, and the two switches for mentions of a role and of everyone.
- The dialog of a workspace shows the level of its administrators as the state holds it. An administrator of the server sets it there; another member reads it. The dialog and the page "Notifications" share one change of that level (ADR 0763).
- The workspace dialog reserves a scrolling status area for a pending default request and the explanation that no default is set, as well as its refusal area. Starting and completing a request keeps the dialog height and the bounds of its controls unchanged (ADR 0730).
- A change is one request that replaces the settings of its scope. One change of a scope is on its way at a time. The dialog shows what the server took until the state of the server holds the events of the change (ADR 0763). A refusal is told in the dialog of the scope, and above the composer when the change came from the menu of a row.
- A channel row opens a menu with a right click, the Menu key, or Shift and F10: the notification settings, and the mute for one hour, for eight hours, until the next day begins in the time zone of the person, or until it is turned off. A muted channel states the end of its mute there and offers "Unmute". The row of a direct conversation has the same menu for its mute (ADR 0515).
- A place is drawn as muted when its summary says so, and when the stored settings say so and their end has not passed: a channel without a message has no summary.

Quiet times:

- "Do not disturb" and the daily quiet time are in the settings of the application. A change is sent to every server on which the device has a session, because ADR 0404 defines them for the whole application and the server stores them per account. A server that does not take the change is named with its reason, and so is a server whose state the device does not hold at that moment, or whose settings of the account are being changed.
- The switch shows "on" when every such server says so. When the servers differ, the settings say so, and the next change sets all of them.
- The daily quiet time is typed as two times of day in a named time zone. The settings show the zone of the stored quiet time, and otherwise the zone of the system; "Change time zone" opens a search of the names of ADR 0798, and a choice is sent with the next change. The settings state the zone of the system when the chosen zone is another one, and state when the build does not know the stored zone. The client sends the times as minutes of a day with the name of the zone (ADR 0412), and applies the quiet time to what it holds by the clock of that zone. Two equal times are refused before a request.

System notifications:

- The state of the application decides what is shown with one function without side effects, and tells it to an interface with two operations: show, and take away by a tag. The binary gives the interface of gpui. Every other start of the application state, such as a test and the tool that draws the screens, gets one that keeps what it was told. No automated run shows a notification or makes the system ask for a permission.
- A notice gets a system notification when all of this holds:
  1. it appeared after the first state of its server in this run of the application, is unread, and is newer than every notice that was seen before;
  2. the account of that server is not in its quiet time now, the state in which the notice appeared is not the one in which "do not disturb" ended, and the notice was not made inside the daily quiet time;
  3. the person did not mute its place;
  4. no window of the application is the active window of the system, or the active window does not show the place of the message. The main window shows the channel or conversation of its main area, and for a reply of a thread also its thread in the side panel; in the view of a call that is the place of the call. The call window shows the place of its call: the voice channel, or the conversation of a direct-message call. Test: `call_notices::the_call_window_shows_the_place_of_the_call_to_the_rules_of_a_system_notification` mentions the person in the chat of the call and in the channel that the main window shows while the call window is the active window: only the second mention gets a system notification. It failed before the call window counted as a window of the application: both got one.
- The level is not evaluated by the client: a notice exists only when the level of its place wants it (ADR 0409).
- A notice waits for the read of its message, at most 32 at one time, and is shown with the author, the place and the start of the text. A message whose read failed is shown without text.
- While the person shares a screen or presents in meeting mode in the call of this device, the account holds its system notifications and the sounds of messages, as Teams does during a presentation: the rule is that of a quiet time, for the notices, the invitations, the requests and the single events of every server; the notices stay in the list and are not shown when the share ends. The choice is `hold_while_presenting` of the account scope (absent: on); the Notifications section has the switch "Hold notifications while you share or present". Tests: `state::notices::tests::a_share_or_a_presentation_holds_the_notifications_of_messages`.
- The account can leave the text of messages out of its system notifications, as Slack and Teams offer: the notification names who wrote and where, and reads "New message". The choice is `hide_text` of the account scope of the notification settings, kept by each server (only the account scope takes it); the Notifications section of the settings shows one switch, "Message text in notifications", and sets every server of the device, as the quiet times do. Tests: `state::notices::tests::a_hidden_text_leaves_the_message_out_of_the_notification`, the window test `notice_settings::the_text_of_a_message_can_be_left_out_of_its_system_notification`.
- The title and the text of a system notification are one line each, without control characters, of at most 120 and 160 characters.
- One server shows at most 3 system notifications within 10 seconds. A notice or an invitation above that bound gets none later, and neither does a notice that waits for the read of its message when the bound is reached: the notices are in the list, and the window draws the invitation.
- An invitation into a call gets a system notification unless the account is in its quiet time or a window of the application is the active window: each window draws the card of the invitation (ADR 0739). It comes before the notices of the same change of the state. An invitation that the first state of a server holds follows the same rule: it waits for an answer at that moment. A call in progress changes no rule: a message in the chat of the call of this device follows rules 1 to 4.
- A join request gets a system notification for a person who may decide it (`approve join requests`), under the rule of an invitation: not in the quiet time of the account and not while a window of the application is the active window, which draws the count of the requests (ADR 0761). The window holds the list of the join requests of every server whose account may decide them. The first list that it holds after the start or after the account got the permission waited before and gets none; while the list is not held (a new connection), what was seen stays, so that a request that arrived meanwhile is new when the list is read again. Its title is "<name> asks to join", its text the name of the server. A press opens the page of the join requests of its server in the main window only; the call window does not open the administration.
- A request of a person outside to enter the call of the account (a locked or full voice channel, ADR 0514) gets a system notification for the people to whom the server gives it, under the same rule; the requests of the first state of a server get none. Its title is "<name> asks to enter <channel>". A press shows the call. Who receives the request is decided by the server; a change of that rule changes no rule here.
- Single events of a server that a person needs to know while no window of the application is active get a system notification, under the rule of an invitation: not in the quiet time of the account, not while a window of the application is active, at most 3 of one server in 10 seconds. The answer to the own request to enter, a removal from the own call, a move of the person and the answer to the own join request are told above that bound: each follows an action on the person, so they cannot arrive as a flood, and the live test met a removal that the bound held back after the start of the call and two answers within 10 seconds (`state::notices::tests::events::a_removal_from_the_call_is_told_above_the_bound_of_notifications`). The list was made from what Discord, Slack and Teams tell and from every event of the protocol (`crates/protocol/src/events.rs`); the other events are told in the window or by a sound (ADR 0707), or change data that no person waits for. The function `state::event_alerts::fact_of` reads what an event means from the event and the state of its server, and `event_alert` applies the rules:
  - the answer to the request of this device to enter a voice channel: "You were let into <channel>" (a press shows the call), "You were not let into <channel>", "Nobody answered your request to enter <channel>" (a press shows the channel); a cancel of the person tells nothing, and another device of the account tells nothing;
  - a move of the person of this device by a moderator: "<name> moved you to <channel>" (a press shows the call);
  - a removal of the person of this device from its call by a moderator: "You were removed from the call in <channel>" (a press shows the channel);
  - a recording that another person starts in the call of this device: "<name> started recording <channel>" (a press shows the call);
  - a recording that the person started is ready, ready with parts missing, or failed, and its transcript is ready or failed: "The recording of <channel> is ready", "The transcript of <channel> is ready" and the other forms; each is told once per recording, whatever later changes of it the server sends (a press shows the voice channel, where the card of the recording is);
  - an export of the person is ready or failed: "Your export of #<channel> is ready" (a press opens the page of the exports);
  - another device was added to the account: "A device was added to your account", with its name; the owner gave a new device access to the account: "The owner gave a new device access to your account" (a press opens the devices or the recoveries of the account in the main window);
  - the account was added to a workspace: "You were added to <workspace>" (a press shows the workspace); the event arrives before the state holds the workspace, so the notification waits up to 60 seconds for its name;
  - a call starts in a voice channel whose level is "all messages" for the person and that is not muted, as Teams tells a meeting that starts in a channel: "A call started in <channel>" (a press shows the channel); not for a person who is in that call;
  - the join request of this device was decided: the state of the access goes from a waiting request to an account ("Your request to join <server> was approved") or to a refusal or an expiry ("... was refused"); a press shows the server.
  A system notification of an event is taken away when the session of its server ended or the server was removed, and when the application forgets it (64 of one server). The answer to a join request of this device comes before the first session of the server or in place of one: it is taken away only with the server. Before this rule the approval was taken away at the next change, in which the server had no state yet. Tests: `state::notices::tests::events::*` (each event, the rules), the window tests `event_alerts::the_answer_to_a_join_request_is_told_while_the_window_is_not_active`, `event_alerts::an_addition_to_a_workspace_is_told_and_leads_to_the_workspace`, `event_alerts::a_recording_an_export_and_a_device_are_told_and_lead_to_their_place` (events given to the state of a real server), and the live test `live::rules::the_events_of_a_call_are_system_notifications_while_no_window_is_active` (a call that starts, a denied and an accepted request to enter, a removal, a move).
- A system notification is taken away when its notice was read or is gone, when its message is gone or deleted, when the state no longer holds the place of its message, when its invitation ended, when its join request or its request to enter was decided, withdrawn or ended, when the session of its server ended or the server was removed, and when the application forgets it: it remembers 64 shown notices of one server. The notifications that are shown when the application ends stay in the system.
- A press on a system notification brings the application to the front, marks the notice as read and shows its message as a row of the list does. A notice whose message could not be read shows its server, and so does an invitation. What a press leads to is kept as long as the notification is shown.
- With more than one saved server the title of the system notification of a notice names its server, and the text of the one of an invitation does.

Windows:

- Windows shows a notification of a program that it did not install from a package only under an application user model identifier that the account registered: the key `Software\Classes\AppUserModelId\<identifier>` of the current user, with the name (`DisplayName`) and the picture (`IconUri`) that the notification shows. The other way, a Start menu shortcut that carries the identifier, would create an entry that the package does not create (ADR 0755), and gpui writes the key at its first notification anyway, with the name only. Without an identifier gpui shows no notification and writes a warning.
- At each start, before the first window, the program writes the picture of 256 pixels (ADR 0756) as `notification-icon.png` into the data directory when the file differs, writes the key of `chat.junto.Junto` with the name `Junto` and the path of that file, and names that identifier for the process (`App::set_app_identity`, which calls `SetCurrentProcessExplicitAppUserModelID`). The code of the key is in `junto-client-core` (`app_identity`), so that the recovery program removes it: `junto-recover unregister` (ADR 0755). A failure to write the key is logged, and the notifications then depend on the key that gpui writes.
- The identifier of the process also groups the taskbar button of the window; ADR 0755 states what the main window gives a pin of the taskbar.
- The key names the picture of the data directory of the last start. A second installation or a second data directory of the same account uses the same identifier.
- gpui activates no application on Windows and on Linux: a press on a notification asks the main window to come to the front, which restores it when it is minimized. On Linux the press carries the activation token that the notification service sends with it, and the window is activated with that token, so that a Wayland compositor gives it the focus (ADR 0752, ADR 0757).

Clients send `Junto-Notification-Preferences: 1` to read and replace `hide_text` and `hold_while_presenting`. Without this capability, snapshots and settings responses omit both fields, and a settings write preserves their stored values atomically. This keeps the strict decoder of earlier clients usable after another device changes these preferences. Requests use a strict settings wrapper; response settings accept added fields. The server regression reads both routes with the earlier decoder and changes the level without losing privacy preferences.

## Consequences

- The notices and notification settings use the components of ADR 0731.
- "Mark all as read" costs one request.
- A thread has no notification setting, because the server has none.
- The settings show the time zone of the daily quiet time, and the person chooses another one from the list of ADR 0798.
- A device with sessions on two servers can hold two values of "do not disturb" after a change on another device. The settings say so.
- The first notice after "do not disturb" ended can miss its system notification when it arrives in the same change of the state as the notices of the quiet time.
- On macOS the system notifications need an application bundle, which the build does not make yet (ADR 0701). `cargo run` shows none.
- On Windows a start writes one key of the registry of the account. A unit test of `junto-client-core` writes the key and the picture below a volatile key of its own under `Software\Classes`, never at the place of `AppUserModelId`, checks that an equal picture is not written again and that another one is, and removes its keys; the check of the Windows package reads the key and the picture after a start of the installed program, removes the key with the recovery program and restores the key of the account as it was before the check (ADR 0915). That a notification appears with the name and the picture, and that a press on it brings the window forward, is checked by a person.
- The window tests against the real server cover a mention that makes a notice and a system notification, the rules for an active window and for a quiet time, the jump from a notice, "mark all as read", the level and the mute of a channel from its menu, the dialogs of the server and of the workspace, and the loss of the notices of a channel with the access to it.
- New direct dependency of the desktop client: `uuid`, for the identifiers of notices and of the scopes of settings. It was in the lock file already.
- New direct dependency of `junto-client-core` on Windows: `windows-registry` 0.6, for the key of the identity. gpui uses the same crate for the same key, so it was in the lock file already.
