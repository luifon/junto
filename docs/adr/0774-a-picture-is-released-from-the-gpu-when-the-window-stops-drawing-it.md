# 0774. A picture is released from the GPU when the window stops drawing it

## Status

Accepted

## Context

A window of the application draws pictures as images of gpui (`RenderImage`): the pictures of a call, the pictures of messages (ADR 0740), the pictures of people (ADR 0783) and the logo of a server (ADR 0799). The first time that a window draws an image, gpui copies its pixels into a texture of the sprite atlas of that window, under the identifier of the image. The copy stays until the image is released with `drop_image`, or until the window closes.

Each picture of a call is a new image with a new identifier: the frame of the display converts the newest picture of each source into an image (ADR 0793). A share of 30 pictures per second gives the window a new image at almost every frame.

`App::drop_image(image, None)` removes the copies from the windows that the application holds at the moment of the call. During the work of a window (a frame of the display, an event, a drawing), gpui takes that window out of the application, so the call skips it. The window replaced the picture of a source in the frame of the display and released the image that it replaced in the same work. The release therefore never reached the window that drew the image: the atlas kept a texture for every picture that the window drew. During a share the GPU memory of the process grew by about 20 MB per second, and it stayed after the share had ended.

## Decision

How a picture of a call reaches the GPU:

- The media engine gives the newest picture of each source (ADR 0793). The follower of the pictures of the window converts the newest picture of each source that has a new one into the form that the window draws, on a blocking thread of the runtime of the client, and the next frame of the display takes it. A window holds one picture per source that it draws: one for the watched share, one small picture for each share, one for each camera.
- The window draws the image with `Window::paint_image`. gpui copies the image into the atlas of the window at the first drawing, and draws the copy until the image is released.
- Each picture of a call is drawn by one element, `DrawnPicture::element()`: a canvas that takes the whole of its parent and has no size of its own. At each paint it places the picture in the largest part of its bounds that has the proportions of the picture (`ObjectFit::Contain`), and paints the image or, on macOS, the pixel buffer there. The stage, the camera tiles, the camera preview of the voice settings and the small pictures of the shares use it, so each picture is drawn inside its area whatever its proportions. The element of gpui `img` is not used for these pictures: it gives its layout node the proportions of the picture as `aspect_ratio`, and taffy 0.13 makes a node without children at least as high as its width divided by that ratio, also when its height is set (`compute_leaf_layout`). In a parent with block layout a picture higher than its area in these proportions was laid out below the area: the bottom of a watched share was cut off, and the camera preview was drawn over the rows below it.
- A camera picture, the picture of the watched share and the small picture of a share have one form in the media engine, `VideoPicture`: planes of brightness and colour at the size at which the window draws the picture. A received buffer in planes (I420) at that size is held as it arrived, through a shared reference; the decoder does not write a buffer that is still held. A buffer in planes of another size is scaled to the size of the drawing from its planes where they are, by `i420_scale` of the vendored `webrtc-sys` (ADR 0733), the scale of libwebrtc with the same filter: libwebrtc 0.3.51 scales only planes that it may change, so a scale through it needed a copy of the planes first. A buffer in another form, such as a pixel buffer of a hardware decoder, is converted into new planes at its full size and then scaled. On macOS the decoders are those of the Objective-C factory of the vendored WebRTC, and its H.264 decoder (VideoToolbox) gives a pixel buffer of Core Video: a received H.264 picture, which a Mac sends for its camera and small shares (ADR 0737), is converted in this way at every frame and is not held without a copy. The cost of that conversion on the receiving Mac was measured with the target `modes` (ADR 0914), whose participants then sent H.264 or VP8 (`JUNTO_TEST_MODES_CODEC`); `docs/client-performance.md` has the figures. In the mode `camera`, with one camera received at 1280 × 720 at 30 pictures a second, a `sample` of 5 seconds gave the conversion into planes (`ObjCFrameBuffer::ToI420`) about 0.3 % of one core, and the write of the planes of the camera pictures into the pixel buffers that the window paints about 5 % with either codec, of which the received picture is about four fifths. A path that paints the pixel buffer of the decoder would remove both for a received H.264 picture, about 4 to 5 % of one core for one camera at that size and less for the smaller layers of a grid of cameras; it is not built, because it needs a new unsafe call that wraps the pixel buffer of the decoder, and a check that the decoder gives the full range that the surface shader reads. The follower makes the form that the window draws from the planes.
- On macOS that form is a pixel buffer of Core Video in the full-range NV12 format of the renderer (the matrix of the surface shader of gpui is full-range BT.601, so the limited-range values of video are expanded when they are written), painted with `Window::paint_surface` (ADR 0701). A mirrored picture of an odd width moves its pixels by one against the columns of colour, which hold one colour value for two pixels: the two pixels of a column of the buffer come from two neighbouring columns of colour of the source. The buffer holds the mean of the two values, as a picture mirrored at its full size and then reduced to one colour value for two pixels has; an even width and a picture that is not mirrored keep each column whole. It does not enter the atlas. At each drawing the renderer makes a Metal texture over the memory of the buffer. The buffer is freed when no picture holds it, so the window keeps no more buffers than it holds pictures.
- gpui has no surface on Windows and Linux. There the follower converts the planes to BGRA once, into the buffer that the image of gpui then holds; the image takes that buffer without a copy.
- A small picture of a share is made once a second at a small size (ADR 0737), and is converted and drawn as a camera picture: on macOS a pixel buffer, on the other systems an image that takes the converted pixels without a copy.

Before, the engine copied each received picture of the watched share into planes, scaled them when the drawing was smaller, converted them to BGRA, copied the pixels from the buffer of the conversion into a shared slice, and the follower copied them again into the image of the window, which gpui copied into a texture of its atlas. Now a picture at the size of the drawing is held without a copy, and a larger one is scaled once from its planes. The timing `timing::the_scale_of_a_picture_with_and_without_a_copy_of_its_planes` of the media engine measures the scale of a picture of 1920 × 1080 to 1128 × 634 with and without the copy; `docs/client-performance.md` has its figures. On macOS the planes are written once into the pixel buffer (one and a half bytes a pixel); on Windows and Linux they are converted once into the image. The conversion of planes at the size of the drawing to BGRA reads the planes where they are.

The pixel buffers are memory of the category "IOSurface" of the process, and the atlas textures are in the category "IOAccelerator (graphics)"; `footprint` states the two apart, and both are in the footprint of the process.

When the GPU copy is released:

- When the window replaces the image of a source with the image of a newer picture.
- When a source ends: a share stops, a camera is turned off, a person with a share leaves the call.
- When the window stops drawing the call: the call moves to the separate call window (ADR 0772) or back, or the call ends.

One function of the application releases an image: `images::release`. It does not call `drop_image` at once: it asks gpui to call it after the current work of the application (`App::defer`). At that time every window is back in the application, and `drop_image` removes the copies from every window. The crate refuses a direct call of `drop_image` with the `disallowed-methods` rule of clippy, so every release goes through this function.

The same function releases the pictures of messages, of people and the logo of a server, which had the same error when a window released them in its own work.

## Consequences

- The GPU memory of the pictures of a call in a window is bounded by the number of sources that the window draws, whatever the number of pictures and the duration of the call. The memory of a source is released when the source ends.
- A replaced image keeps its GPU copy until the current work of the application ends. The window does not draw it in that time: it holds the newer image.
- Tests:
  - `calls::the_watched_share_is_drawn_in_the_form_of_a_camera_picture` in the window tests: on macOS the window holds the watched share and no image of gpui for it; on the other systems it holds one image with an entry in the atlas. On every system the window paints at its next drawing a picture of the size of the share inside the bounds of the picture of the share, with its proportions (the paint of a picture is recorded while the test holds a record of the paints, which only the feature `test-support` offers; without a record no paint is kept, so the measurement targets, which are built with that feature, hold nothing of it. Test: `ui::picture::tests::a_paint_is_kept_only_while_a_record_lives`).
  - `calls::a_very_wide_share_fits_the_stage_at_each_size_of_the_window` (a share of a screen of 3440 × 1440, 21:9, watched while the main window takes six sizes from 800 × 1000 to 5120 × 1440, then in the call window in full screen at four sizes) and `calls::a_16_9_share_fits_a_very_wide_stage` (1920 × 1080 in windows from 3440 × 600 to 5120 × 1440, where the stage is more than twice as wide as high) in the window tests: after each change of the size, the first drawing paints the picture inside the picture area, the area inside the stage, with the proportions of the share, reaching two edges of the area.
  - `calls::a_portrait_share_is_drawn_inside_a_wide_stage` (a share of 820 × 1048 in a window of 1600 × 900) and `calls::a_landscape_share_is_drawn_inside_a_narrow_and_short_stage` (1512 × 1048 in 860 × 540) in the window tests: the painted picture lies inside the picture area of the stage, has the proportions of the share and reaches the two edges of the area in one direction. `device_checks::the_camera_preview_is_drawn_inside_its_area`: the camera preview of 640 × 360 is painted inside its area of height 160 in the same way. `calls::a_portrait_camera_is_drawn_inside_a_wide_tile`: a camera picture of 36 × 64 in a wide call tile is painted inside the tile in the same way. With the layout of `img` (the canvas given the proportions of the picture as `aspect_ratio`) these four tests fail on Linux. `calls::a_portrait_avatar_is_drawn_in_its_square_in_a_tile`: an avatar of 48 × 200 set through the API is laid out as a square in its tile; the window holds the middle square of each avatar (`state::people::decode`), and the avatar element states the square as its proportions.
  - `prepared::tests::the_watched_share_is_converted_in_the_form_of_a_camera_picture` and `share::tests::an_image_holds_the_pixels_that_the_conversion_wrote` in the unit tests of the client: the follower makes a pixel buffer on macOS and an image elsewhere, and the image holds the buffer that the conversion wrote.
  - `engine::share::tests::a_picture_at_the_size_of_the_drawing_holds_the_received_planes` and `share::picture::tests::a_received_frame_in_planes_at_the_size_of_the_drawing_is_held_without_a_copy` in the unit tests of the media engine: a picture at the size of the drawing reads the planes of the received buffer, and a smaller drawing makes planes of its size. `share::picture::tests::the_pixel_buffer_has_the_colours_of_the_bgra_picture_at_an_odd_size` (macOS): every pixel of the buffer at 63 × 35 pixels, mirrored and not, has the brightness of its source pixel, and each column of colour the mean of the colour values of the source pixels of its two pixels; where those two values are equal, the values, read with the matrix of the surface shader, give the colours of the BGRA picture within 4 of 255. The mirrored buffer took the colour of one source column for both pixels before, which the comparison of every pixel finds and the earlier samples of even columns did not.
  - `calls::the_small_pictures_of_the_shares_are_drawn_in_the_form_of_a_camera_picture` in the window tests: on macOS the window holds no image of gpui for the two small pictures of the bar of shares; on the other systems it holds two images. On every system it paints each picture, a portrait one and a landscape one, inside the frame of its small picture, with its proportions; the frame is a flex container, which taffy lays out without the proportions of the picture, so this test also passes with the layout of `img`. `share::picture::tests::held_planes_are_scaled_as_the_call_library_scales_them` in the unit tests of the media engine: the scale from held planes gives the planes of the scale of libwebrtc, byte for byte; `share::picture::tests::held_planes_drawn_smaller_are_not_copied_first`: a picture in planes that is drawn smaller makes no new planes of the whole picture (a count of the calls of `planes` in the test build).
  - `calls::a_window_holds_gpu_images_only_for_the_pictures_that_it_draws_now` in the window tests, on the test platform of gpui: forty pictures of each source (the watched share, two small pictures, two cameras) are drawn in the main window and then in the separate call window. Each window then has atlas entries only for the images that it holds now, and none after the shares and the cameras end. Every replaced picture is freed, and on macOS every pixel buffer of a replaced picture has no holder other than the test; after the end of the shares and the cameras, none is held. It runs in the ordinary suite, without a device.
  - The test target `frames` (macOS, with `JUNTO_TEST_FRAMES=1`): two native windows drawn by Metal, in a call with the media server of the tests, both sharing the synthetic screen and showing the synthetic camera of both people. The windows get the frames of a display for 20 seconds with the call in the main window and then in the separate call window. The program reads with `footprint` the memory of the pictures of the process, the sum of the categories "IOAccelerator (graphics)" and "IOSurface", and fails when it grows by more than 48 MiB in that time, or stays more than 48 MiB above its value from before the shares after they have ended.
