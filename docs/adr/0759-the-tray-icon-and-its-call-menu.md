# 0759. The tray icon and its call menu

## Status

Accepted. Unit tests cover the derivation of the state, the pictures (`junto-icon`: `a_colour_tray_picture_is_a_disc_of_its_state_with_a_white_sign`, `a_template_tray_picture_is_its_sign_in_black_on_transparent`, `the_mention_mark_is_a_dot_at_the_upper_right_corner_in_each_state_and_style`; `junto-client`: `tray::tests::the_build_rendered_each_picture`), the tooltip with the mentions and the menu; the window test `tray::the_unread_mentions_add_the_mark_and_keep_the_menu` covers mentions sent by another person reaching the mark and the tooltip, and reading the server removing them. A window test drives the kept tray through a call without a media server; a test of the window live tier presses the menu entries in a real call. On Linux, tests with a private session bus cover the registration with a watcher, a watcher that starts later, the properties of the item, the layout of the menu and the presses. On Windows, unit tests cover the icon from the pixels and the one icon per thread. The macOS status item is compiled and run only by the macOS CI job. The icon and the menu on a real desktop are checked by a person (see the list below).

## Context

A person in a call often has the window of Junto behind other windows. Other chat applications show the state of the call in the area of the system for status icons and offer the call actions in a menu there. Each system has its own interface for that area: the status bar of AppKit on macOS, `Shell_NotifyIconW` on Windows, and on Linux the StatusNotifierItem specification over D-Bus, which a desktop supports only when a program owns `org.kde.StatusNotifierWatcher` on the session bus. gpui provides none of them.

## Decision

- The crate `junto-client` has a module `tray`. The state of the application derives a `TrayState` from the calls as it has taken them, at each change, and tells it to a `SystemTray` only when it differs from the state that was told last. `TrayState` holds no call, or the call with three marks: the person closed the microphone, a moderator or meeting mode holds the microphone closed, the person turned the sound off; the seat of the call (ADR 0788); and the unread mentions that the application counts.
- Each state has a picture of its own, readable at the size of a menu bar, as Discord's (the application icon with a small mark over it was not readable there): outside a call, three rings in the places of the discs of the application icon; in a call, a whole microphone; a crossed microphone when the person closed the microphone; a crossed microphone with a lock when a moderator or meeting mode holds it closed; crossed headphones when the sound is off, which takes precedence. The tooltip names the state: "Junto", "Junto: in a call", "Junto: in a call, microphone muted", "Junto: in a call, microphone closed by the call", "Junto: in a call, sound off".
- While the application counts an unread message for the person, every picture has the mention mark, a dot at its upper right corner, and the tooltip adds the number ("…, 2 unread mentions and direct messages"). The number is the count of the badge of the application, `AppState::for_person` (ADR 0741): the mentions of the channels and the unread messages of the direct conversations of every server, as the server book counts them. It reaches the tray through `AppState::set_tray_mentions` at each change of the state of the application; a change of the number alone keeps the menu and its generation.
- macOS: the pictures are template pictures, the sign in black on transparent, and the menu bar draws them in its own colour on a light and on a dark bar. The mention mark is cut out of the sign with a gap around it. Windows and Linux: the sign is white on a disc of the colour of the state (green in a call, cinnabar for a closed microphone and for the sound off, a dark ochre for a microphone that the call holds closed), and outside a call the picture is the body of the application icon, as Discord shows its own; the mention mark is a red dot with a white ring.
- The pictures are not committed. The signs are SVG files in `packaging/icon/tray/`: the rings, and the Lucide drawings of the window with a wider stroke. The crate `junto-icon` composes and renders each picture (`junto_icon::tray`). The build script of the client renders every state with and without the mention mark: on macOS template PNG pictures of 18 and 36 pixels, one image with a representation for each scale of the display, shown at 18 points; on Windows and Linux straight RGBA pixels at 16, 20, 22, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 48 and 64 pixels, the small icon of Windows from 100 to 300 % of scale and the sizes that Linux status areas ask for. The build of the client on macOS therefore compiles the renderer too (ADR 0756). `junto-icon tray <state> <mention|none> <template|colour> <size> <file>` writes one picture.
- The menu, in a call: "Mute microphone" or "Unmute microphone", "Turn sound off" or "Turn sound on", "Leave the call"; then, after a separator, "Open Junto" and "Quit". Outside a call only the last two. The text of each entry follows the state. "Unmute microphone" is disabled while the server holds the microphone closed, as the button of the window is.
- An entry does what the control of the window does: `use_microphone` with the action of the menu, which opens the microphone only at the seat that the menu showed; `set_sound_off`; `leave_call`; "Open Junto" brings the main window to the front as a press on a notification does; "Quit" runs the same function as the `Quit` action, and the application leaves the call before it ends. A primary press on the icon on Windows and Linux opens Junto; on macOS a press on the item opens the menu, as status items there do.
- Each kind of entry has a number (microphone 1, sound 2, leave 3, open 4, quit 5). The menu has a generation that changes with each change of the call or of its marks, and the code of an entry is the generation and the kind (`entry_code`). A system can draw a menu and report its press after the state changed: a code of a menu of an earlier generation names no entry, and the press does nothing. So an "Unmute" drawn in one call does not open the microphone in the call that the person was moved to, and a "Mute" does not become "Unmute" (ADR 0788). The code of each system reports only the code of the pressed entry. Test: `tray::tests::a_press_on_a_menu_of_an_earlier_state_is_refused`.
- The presses arrive on the thread of the system code and reach the thread of the windows through a channel, where `tray::press` calls the state of the application.
- macOS: an `NSStatusItem` of the system status bar, with an `NSMenu` whose entries call the method `pressed:` of a target object; the code is the tag of the entry. The class of that object is defined with `define_class!`, which needs unsafe code; it is in the module `menu_bar` of `junto-client-core`, the one module of that crate that allows unsafe code on macOS.
- Windows: `Shell_NotifyIconW` with version 4 of the icon messages and a hidden top-level window of the thread of the windows, in the module `notification_area` of `junto-client-core`, which allows unsafe code. The message loop of gpui gives the messages of that window to its procedure. A secondary press or the menu key shows a popup menu at the point that the shell gives; the window becomes the foreground window first, so that the menu closes at a press outside it. The window is top-level, not a window for messages only, so that it receives the message `TaskbarCreated` of a new shell, after which the icon is added again. The picture is the smallest rendering at least as large as the small icon size of the system. One icon exists per thread. The application takes the icon away when it quits, and it is not added again after that: the shell keeps the icon of an ended process until the pointer passes over it.
- Linux: a StatusNotifierItem at `/StatusNotifierItem` with the bus name `org.kde.StatusNotifierItem-<process>-1`, and a `com.canonical.dbusmenu` menu at `/MenuBar`, served with zbus on a worker of its own, as the desktop notifications are (ADR 0752). The pictures are given as `IconPixmap` in every rendered size; the item emits `NewIcon` and `NewToolTip`, and the menu `LayoutUpdated` with a new revision, at each change. The identifier of a menu entry is its code. The item registers with the watcher when it starts and again with each new owner of the watcher name, so a desktop that starts its watcher later, or starts it again, shows the icon. Without a watcher there is no icon: the worker logs this once for each connection and nothing fails. A lost connection to the session bus is logged once, and the worker connects again after 5 seconds with the state of now.
- Only the start of the binary (`app::state`) installs the tray of the system. Every other start, such as a test and the screen tool `junto-screens`, gets a `KeptTray`, which shows nothing and keeps each state that it was told. Its test hook presses the enabled entry of the shown menu with a given label, through the same `tray::press` as the system trays.

## Consequences

- A desktop on Linux without a StatusNotifierWatcher, such as GNOME without an extension for these icons, shows no tray icon. The application works without it.
- The macOS pictures follow the light and the dark menu bar; the state is shown by the sign alone there.
- The sign fills three quarters of the disc of the colour style, so that at 16 pixels it is 12 pixels wide; the colour of the disc also states the call state there.
- A person checks on each system at the live test:
  - The icon is visible in the menu bar of macOS, the notification area of Windows (possibly in the overflow area) and the status area of a Linux desktop with a watcher (KDE Plasma, or GNOME with an extension for these icons).
  - The picture and the tooltip change when the person joins a call, mutes, turns the sound off, turns it on, unmutes and leaves.
  - Each entry works: "Mute microphone" and "Unmute microphone", "Turn sound off" and "Turn sound on", "Leave the call", "Open Junto" with the main window minimized and behind other windows, and "Quit" during a call (the other people see the person leave).
  - On Windows and Linux a primary press on the icon opens Junto; on Windows the menu closes at a press outside it, and the icon comes back after Explorer is restarted.
  - The icon goes when the application ends.
