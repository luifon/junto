# 0731. Theme tokens, components, the interface scale and the rule for a narrow window

## Status

Accepted

## Context

ADR 0703 fixes one theme in one file, an interface scale from 90% to 150%, and a text catalog, and leaves open which panel gives way when the window is narrow. ADR 0702 requires a keyboard path and a label for every control. Screens share the theme and components in `crates/client/src/theme.rs` and `crates/client/src/ui/`; icons and fonts are in `crates/client/assets/`. The screen renderer provides reproducible pictures from this code without a system window.

## Decision

Theme:

- One value, the theme, holds every colour, the three font families, the text styles, the radii and the fixed measures of the layout. Drawing code names no colour and no font.
- A measure is given in logical pixels at 100% and becomes rems. The size of one rem is 16 pixels times the interface scale. A border is one pixel at every scale.
- The fonts are carried in the binary: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for text and controls, with its italic faces for the italic text of a message (ADR 0740), Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for addresses and codes, Marcellus for titles of 18 pixels and more. Each is under the SIL Open Font License, and its licence text is beside its files.
- The icons are SVG files in the binary. An icon is drawn in one colour that the caller gives: gpui draws the alpha of the SVG as a mask in the colour of the element.
- The icons are the icons of Lucide, release 1.61.0, under the ISC licence (ADR 0801). One set gives every icon the same grid, line and corners. The files are in `crates/client/assets/icons/lucide/`, unchanged and under their Lucide names, so that a newer release replaces them file for file; the licence file of Lucide is beside them. Their form: a grid of 24 units, an outline with a line of 2 units, round ends and round joins, in `currentColor`, which the SVG library of gpui draws in black with full alpha.
- The microphone that another person closed (`mic-held.svg`) has no Lucide icon. It is drawn in the same form from the Lucide icons `mic-off` and `lock`: the microphone is moved 2 units to the left, the lock is at the lower right as on the lock icons of Lucide, and the parts of the microphone that would touch the lock are removed.
- The four buttons of the title strip on Windows and Linux (minimize, maximize, restore, close) keep their own drawings of 16 units with a line of 1 unit, the thin line of the buttons of the system (ADR 0703).
- An icon names the action of its place, as in other chat applications: a telephone for a direct call, for its states and to leave a call; headphones only for the sound output; a cog for settings; a shield for roles and permissions; one person for a direct conversation and a contact, two people for members, a person in a circle for the account; a smile with a plus to add a reaction; a folder to show a file in its folder; a double check to mark as read. A button with two states shows the icon of its state: the microphone, the sound output and the camera show a slashed icon while off, and the share of the screen shows a slashed icon while a share starts or runs, when a press stops it.
- Tests: `assets::tests::every_icon_is_a_drawing_with_one_colour_that_the_source_finds` (the root element of every Lucide-form file has the attributes of Lucide, its shapes have only shape attributes and `currentColor`; the window buttons have one colour), `every_file_of_the_icons_is_the_drawing_of_one_icon` (no file without an icon, no icon without a file, no other file than the Lucide licence) and `the_licence_of_lucide_is_beside_its_icons`.
- No interface text is smaller than 12 pixels, and running text has at least 14 (ADR 0703). A test holds both.

Generated avatars:

- An avatar without an image is a square of tiles that is mirrored on both axes: 5 by 5 tiles in three colours for a person and a server, 3 by 3 tiles in two colours for a workspace.
- The pattern and the colour set follow from a hash of the identifier, so an account has the same avatar on every device. Every colour of the set is in the pattern.
- The edges of the tiles lie on pixels of the display, and the line between two tiles has at least one such pixel.

Components:

- Each component has one definition: icon, avatar, tessera band and selection mark, level meter, button, text field, segmented control, switch, choice box, tab, volume control, badge, dot, chip, flag, recording mark, status line, the rows of the two lists, the tile of a person in a call, category header, section label, panel header, notice, notice slot, wait box, state panel, menu, dialog, tooltip and scroll area.
- A control is a tab stop, is activated with Enter or Space, has a role and a label for the accessibility tree, and shows a mark while it holds the keyboard focus: a ring of 2 pixels, in terracotta on stone and in ochre on the server book. A text field shows its caret and the border colour of its focus. A disabled control is no tab stop.
- A dialog and a menu hold the keyboard focus while they are open. Tab stays inside, the arrow keys move between the items of a menu, Escape closes, and the focus returns to where it was. A click outside a menu and on the cover of a dialog closes it. A dialog that must be answered has no close control. When the control that holds the focus leaves the dialog, as a button does that is disabled while its action runs, the focus returns to the dialog. A dialog that is taller than its place scrolls its content, and when the keyboard moves the focus to a control of the content outside the view, the content scrolls by the least distance that shows the whole control with its border and its focus mark, or its top when the control is taller than the view. A focus that a press of the pointer gives scrolls nothing: the control is in view, and a scroll between the press and the release would move it away from the pointer. A scroll of the person afterwards is not undone while the focus stays on that control. gpui tells no element where the focused element is drawn, so each focusable component (button, text field, switch, choice box, tab, volume control, segmented control, stepper and the rows of the lists) draws, only while it holds the focus, one element that reports its bounds, and the dialog reads the report of its content after the content. A component keeps its focus handle while it is disabled, so the focus can return to it. Tests: `ui::dialog::tests::tab_in_a_tall_dialog_shows_the_focused_control`, `a_press_on_a_button_partly_in_view_scrolls_nothing`, `only_the_focused_control_draws_its_report`, `a_disabled_button_keeps_its_focus_handle`.
- The menu of a server in the collapsed server book opens when the pointer is over the icon and stays for 220 milliseconds after the pointer left, so that the pointer can move into it. Enter on the icon opens the same menu and moves the focus into it.
- A notice slot reserves its height even when empty and scrolls its contents independently. Its scroll position belongs to its element identifier, so repeated rows give it a distinct parent scope. Views choose a fixed height for their notice area as described in ADR 0730.
- The text field takes its editing from the text input of the component library and its frame, colours and type from the theme.

Scale and narrow window:

- The interface scale is one of 90, 100, 110, 125 and 150 percent. It is a setting of the installation in `settings.json` (ADR 0710), with the form of the server book. A change of one setting is written as a change of that setting, under the lock of the data directory. The window uses the new value at once. When the write fails, the settings dialog says so until that setting is in the file, and the next change of any setting writes it again.
- The layout reacts to the width of the window in logical pixels, which is its width divided by the scale. Below 1200 logical pixels the window is narrow.
- In a narrow window the server book collapses to icons, and the side panel lies over the content at the right edge, with a flat cover behind it; a click on the cover closes the panel. The person can expand the server book in a narrow window; that choice lasts until the window is wide again.
- The channel column keeps its width at every scale.
- The server book, the channel column, each side panel (thread, pinned messages, notifications, search, members, transcript) and the panel of the call in the main window and in the separate call window are made wider or narrower by a drag of their edge at the side of the main area, as in Discord, Slack and Teams. The edge is a band of 8 design pixels over the border, with the cursor of a horizontal resize; it holds the keyboard focus, and the Left and Right keys move it by 16 design pixels. Each panel has its own bounds in design pixels (`Panel::bounds`: the server book 160 to 360, the channel column 200 to 420, the other panels 280 to 720), and in the window as it is the main area keeps at least 360 design pixels and the stage of the separate call window 240; a panel is never narrower than its width of the design because of the window, and a window too narrow for it shows the side panel over the content as above. The width of each panel is kept in the settings of the installation (`settings.json`, `panel_widths`) at the end of a drag or at a key, and is used again after a restart; a panel that the person never changed has the width of the design. A collapsed server book has no edge. Tests: `panel_widths::the_edge_of_a_panel_changes_its_width_within_its_bounds_and_the_width_is_kept` drags the edges of the server book, the channel column and the member list, reaches the least width of the member list, sees that the notifications keep their own width, and starts the application again; `panel_widths::the_edge_of_a_panel_moves_with_the_arrow_keys`.

Pictures of the screens:

- The tool `junto-screens` draws each screen and state with the real window code on gpui's test platform and its headless renderer, and writes PNG files. It opens no window and takes no keyboard focus. A developer compares the files with the layout and component rules of this record and the screen records. Each run also measures every screen: the components of the window name the areas whose parts start at their top and follow each other (each scrolled list, the content of a dialog, a state panel; `ui::area`), the drawing records their bounds under `test-support`, and in the picture of the screen no band of the ground colour of an area is taller than 40 design pixels above its first part or between two parts, and an area has at least one part (`gaps::empty_bands`, ADR 0730). A screen is drawn until two frames in a row lay out the same areas, at most 8 frames, and a screen whose drawing takes longer than 120 seconds ends the program with the name of the screen (a task that never ends kept `run_until_parked` from returning, and a run once drew for 34 minutes without an end). The run ends with an error that lists every empty band. `JUNTO_SCREENS_FILES=0` measures without writing the pictures; the Linux CI job runs it (ADR 0905).

## Consequences

- The dark theme is a second value of the theme (`Theme::broadcast`) with other colours and the measures, fonts and text styles of the light theme. A colour that a screen needs and the theme lacks is added to the theme. `junto-screens` draws every screen in one theme per run: the light theme, or the dark theme with `JUNTO_SCREENS_THEME=dark`. The parts of a switch have a contrast of at least 3 to 1 with what is next to them, and a button filled with an accent has another ground under the pointer, in both themes; a unit test holds both. The chosen part of a segmented control has another ground and a mark of 2 pixels in the accent colour at its foot, with a contrast of at least 3 to 1 to both grounds in both themes (open item RP8: in the dark theme only a slightly lighter ground and bold text showed the choice).
- At 150% in a window of 1440 pixels the layout has 960 logical pixels and is narrow.
- A window of 1320 pixels at 100% is wide and gives the content 544 pixels while the side panel is open.
- The width of the scroll bar and of a border does not follow the scale.
- The drawing of a screen can be checked against the layout and component rules on a machine without a display. The window controls of the operating system are not in such a picture.
- Generated avatars derive their patterns from the identifier.
- A newer release of Lucide is taken by copying its files over those of `crates/client/assets/icons/lucide/` with its licence file. Lucide renames icons between releases and keeps the old name as an alias for a time; a renamed icon changes its file name in the list of `assets.rs`, and the unit test fails while a file and the list differ.
- An image that an account has set as its avatar is shown in place of the generated avatar when the window holds it (ADR 0783).
