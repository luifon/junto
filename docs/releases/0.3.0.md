# Junto 0.3.0

Junto is a self-hosted application for team text chat, voice and video calls, screen sharing, and call recording with transcripts. Version 0.3.0 follows 0.2.1. It changes the screens of the whole window and has database migrations: back up the server before the upgrade, and run the check of the new release first (see "From 0.2.1").

## What is new

### Rules for the whole window

- A control that your permissions do not allow is absent, not shown disabled; a control that is disabled for a passing reason states the reason.
- Every row with a menu shows the same "⋮" button while the pointer is over it, while it has the keyboard focus and while its menu is open. The Menu key and Shift+F10 open the same menu.
- A press on a control inside a row does only that control's action: it no longer selects the row, starts a drag or selects text.
- The buttons of the headers are icons with a tooltip. Menu items show the keys bound to their action.
- The words of the window follow one glossary, and many long sentences are shorter. The floating copy button is gone; values that you copy have a copy button beside them.

### Server list and Server settings

- Each server has a menu: the "⋮" of its row, a right click on its row or icon, and the item "Server menu" of the hover menu of a collapsed server. Its groups are Arrange and "On this device"; "Keep connected in the background" is a check item.
- The workspaces have a menu of their own at the arrow of the header and at their row.
- "Add server or workspace" opens one window with the tabs Server and Workspace. The address of an invite page that a browser shows is read as an invite.
- One update notice: the version line has its update button beside the version.
- Administration is now "Server settings", one window per server with three groups: Server, the chosen workspace (with a selector that does not change the main window), and "Your account". Every member has "Your account": Profile, Notifications, Linked devices, Recoveries, Exports, Access tokens, and Leave or delete. The separate "Your account" window is gone.
- The settings of a channel, a category and a person open inside Server settings, with a line back to their page and tabs for their pages.
- People and Roles have a "⋮" menu on each row. A role has the tabs Appearance, Permissions and Members; Members adds and removes holders.
- A channel's permissions start with "Private channel" and "Who can see this channel"; the rules of each role and person are under "Advanced permissions".
- A workspace no longer has permission rules for single members. The upgrade turns each such rule into a role of that member (see "From 0.2.1").

### Text channels and messages

- A message has one set of actions for its bar and its menu: three recent emoji, Add a reaction, Reply, Reply in thread, Forward, Copy text and Edit, and the rest in "⋮". Delete is shown in the colour of an error.
- A message is edited in its row, with the formatting row, Write and Preview, and completion of mentions and emoji. Enter saves, Escape cancels.
- A forward can carry a comment of its own. The forward dialog offers the followed threads of every workspace of the server, with a row that reads more of them, and a pasted message link chooses its thread.
- One read item follows the state of a place: "Mark as read" while it has unread messages, "Mark as unread" while it has none.
- Search starts in the shown channel and has its filters as one line of chips.
- The member list of a channel lists the people who can see it.
- Threads: the actions of a thread are in its header, and the entry "Threads" at the top of the channel column lists the threads that you follow in the workspace.
- The notices of a server are named "Activity" and open from a bell beside the search field.
- "Channel settings" is in the menu of a channel; the header of a channel has no settings button.
- Conversations have "Call" and "People" (or the profile of the other person) in their header, and a group conversation may have no name: it is named by its people.

### Calls and recordings

- One "Join call": a press joins at once, and its small arrow offers "Join after a device check". "Call" of a conversation and the answer to an invitation work the same way.
- A row of your own controls lies under the stage in both windows: microphone, sound, camera, share, raise hand, View, the devices and Leave.
- "Room settings" on the call bar holds meeting mode, the lock, the user limit and "Hide who is in the call". The section "Call" of Settings is gone.
- The tab of the people of a call is named "In the call", with one line per person and "Find a member".
- A thread, the pins and a transcript of the channel of the call open in the right column of the call. Pins, Search and the Activity bell stand in a row under its tabs.
- Speaking marks are shown only for the people of your own call.
- When you talk while you are muted, Junto tells you, with Unmute; the notice can be turned off.
- Screen share: "No sound" and the only screen are preselected, the quality is chosen in the dialog, and your own share line has "Stop sharing".
- "Record" starts at once with the default language of the server; its arrow chooses another language. The Files panel of a voice channel has a tab "Recordings".
- On Windows, the taskbar preview of Junto has microphone, sound and leave-call buttons during a call.

### Status and notifications

- A press on your own picture or name opens your menu: the four status choices and a status text such as "Out of office", with an end time. Others see the text in the member lists and on your profile card.
- Your notification levels for a server, its workspaces and the channels with a setting of their own are a page "Notifications" in "Your account" of Server settings. The menus of the server and of the workspace open it. The "Do not disturb" switch for all servers is gone: "Do not disturb" is a status of each server.

### Linking a device

- A new device is linked with one code of eight characters. The device that you already use types it, compares the device code, chooses the servers and approves; the new device then logs in on each of them. The first start of Junto offers "I use Junto on another computer".

### Settings

- Settings has the sections Name and picture for new servers, Appearance (with "Start and close"), Voice and video, Sounds, Notifications (with the daily quiet time), Keyboard shortcuts, Linked devices, Permissions and About.
- The push-to-talk key is set in Keyboard shortcuts only.
- The sounds are in groups, and each permission row says how to give the permission.

## Server changes

### Migrations

0.3.0 applies seven migrations to a database of 0.2.1, in this order:

- 0059: removes the workspace permission rules of single members (after the start has turned each of them into a role, see below).
- 0060: the status text of an account and its end time.
- 0061: the relays that link a new device to every server of the account.
- 0062: the default spoken language of the recordings of the server (English).
- 0063: the longest stretch of missing sound of a recording, and whether the job of the composite joined the room.
- 0064: the comment of a forward. This migration rebuilds the search column of the message table, so it takes longer on a server with many messages.
- 0065: a group conversation may have no name.

### Per-member workspace rules

A workspace no longer has permission rules for one member. At the first start of 0.3.0, each such rule becomes a role of that member at the bottom of the order of roles, named "Rules of" and the member's name, so that every member keeps the permissions that the member has now.

Before the upgrade, run `check-upgrade` of the new release against the running database, as step 0 of "Upgrade" in `docs/install.md` describes. It lists each case that needs a decision:

- a member who would gain a permission that a rule of the member denies now;
- a member with "Manage roles" who would stand above a role made from the rule of a member who stands at or above them now;
- a former member who still has a rule;
- a workspace or a member that would hold more roles than the server allows.

Handle each listed case in the running server, for example by changing or removing the rule or the roles of that member, and run the check again until it lists none. While a case is listed, the new server does not start, makes no copy of the database and changes nothing.

### API

- The routes of the permission rules of one member (`/api/v1/workspaces/{workspace_id}/members/{account_id}/permissions`) are retired: they stay in API version 1 as deprecated and answer `410` with the code `retired`.
- The route `PUT /api/v1/call/speaking` and the event `call_speaking` are removed: the server keeps no speaking state.
- New: the status text of an account, the relay of a device link, the followed threads of a workspace, the recording settings of the server, and the comment of a forward. The event revision is 25.

### Compatibility

- Clients of 0.2.x work with a server of 0.3.0. They receive the name of a group without a name as the names of its other members. They show no speaking marks outside their call; their speaking reports get "not found" and change nothing. The page of the rules of a member is refused as retired.
- A client of 0.3.0 works with a server of 0.2.x and keeps its group conversations. The features that need the server of 0.3.0, such as the status text, the link of a device on every server with one code, the Threads list and the comment of a forward, are not offered by such a server.

## Dependencies

- `rustix`, `url` and `zip` are updated to versions without known advisories.
- `chrono` and `chrono-tz` are marked unmaintained by their advisories (RUSTSEC-2026-0543, RUSTSEC-2026-0555), with no fixed version and no defect named. They are accepted until a review on 2027-02-23; `chrono-tz`, which the client uses for named time zones, is to be replaced.

## Supported systems

- macOS 26 on Apple Silicon. Older versions of macOS are not blocked and are not tested.
- Windows 11 on x86-64.
- Linux on x86-64 with PipeWire, under X11 or Wayland, with glibc 2.35 or newer.

## Packages

- macOS: `Junto-0.3.0-macos-aarch64.dmg`, a disk image with `Junto.app` and a link to the Applications folder, and `Junto-0.3.0-macos-aarch64.zip`, which holds `Junto.app`.
- Windows: `Junto-0.3.0-windows-x86_64-setup.exe`, a setup program that installs Junto for the account without administrator rights, with an entry in the Start menu and in the list of installed programs, and `Junto-0.3.0-windows-x86_64.zip`, which holds the folder `Junto`. `Junto-0.3.0-windows-x86_64.pdb` holds the symbols of `junto.exe`; it resolves the frames of an error report of the Windows client and is not needed to run Junto.
- Linux: `Junto-0.3.0-x86_64.AppImage`, `junto-0.3.0-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` with the directory `junto`, and `junto_0.3.0_amd64.deb`.

`SHA256SUMS` holds the SHA-256 of each file.

## First start

Junto has no paid code signature.

- macOS blocks the first open of a downloaded copy, and the dialog has no button that opens Junto. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, choose "Open Anyway" for Junto, then confirm.
- On Windows, Microsoft Defender SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC" at the first start. Choose "More info", then "Run anyway". Smart App Control must be off; with it on, Windows does not run Junto.
- On Linux, the AppImage needs `chmod +x` and `fusermount` of FUSE 2 or 3; without FUSE, start it with `--appimage-extract-and-run`. Install the `.deb` with `sudo apt install ./junto_0.3.0_amd64.deb`.

## From 0.2.1

Clients of 0.2.x update themselves to 0.3.0: Update on the notice, or Install update in Settings, Version. The `.deb` is installed by hand with `sudo apt install ./junto_0.3.0_amd64.deb`.

The server is upgraded as the section "Upgrade" of `docs/install.md` describes: first `check-upgrade` of the new release against the running database (step 0, see "Per-member workspace rules"), then a backup. 0.3.0 migrates the database, so the server of 0.2.1 cannot be started again on the migrated database; the way back is the copy that the server makes before the migration, or the backup.

## Updates

Junto installs updates from inside the application: Update on its notice, or Install update in Settings, Version. It reads them from the GitHub releases of `https://github.com/luifon/junto` and verifies them with the keys of the project. The `.deb` is the exception: Junto cannot update a system package, so a newer version is installed with `sudo apt install` and the `.deb` of that version.

## Server

The server is installed, backed up and upgraded as `docs/install.md` of the source repository describes.
